The creator prefers making cards stronger to making them weaker, and would like every card to be worth playing ("positive EV"). Still raise nerfs when the numbers call for one, and let the creator decide.

Lift is generally fine as a measure, but a card with a special ability can be worth more than its lift shows. A card that makes its theme deck strong is strong even if its random-deck lift is modest. Ambrel lampwright was the example: it makes its theme deck too good, so it went to 2 / 3 (2026-11-15). A 1 / 3 with a real ability can be worth its cost.

Many out-of-band cards only work through a mechanic: they add value to a theme deck, not on their own. Some aren't meant to be a body at all, just a means to an end for another card. Black decks, for example, would love a horrible cheap card they can sacrifice easily (2026-11-15). Don't "fix" an enabler or fodder card by giving it a body or stats, and don't read its low random-deck lift as a flaw. Judge it by what it does for its deck.

Bot misplays understate some cards. Rimebolt is played to set up a kill: 2 damage plus the freeze lets a 2 / 2 beat a 2 / 3 on the next attack. Trust the creator's play experience over a low bot score.

Some decks are meant to be strong: Sun and cinder is supposed to be overpowered (2026-11-15).

**Why:** creator feedback on the 2026-11-15 voting page and its follow-up; they rejected most nerfs and many plain stat bumps.

**How to apply:** when a card misses its band, propose a buff to the weak cards first and offer a nerf as the option, not the default. Check theme-deck strength alongside lift. Related: [[breachline-deck-goals]], [[quick-balance-check]], [[mechanic-verdicts]].

When writing Breachline lore in an in-world voice (the lore codex, and similar), follow these points:
- **Assume the everyday.** Magic, healing and revival are common knowledge, so they appear only in passing ("the rest deserved their rest"). Explain only what a person in the world would find remarkable.
- **The writer is a Gnosis scholar of Ambrel** who catalogues the world like a historian and quotes the people they've met, with short credits.
- **Describe groups as you'd describe political parties or a neighbouring country:** their temperament, where they live, what people think of them. Not policy lists, and not a restatement of the setting's premise.
- **Keep caveats and looks short**, and leave background in the background.
- **The Dominion should feel frightening.**
- **Hint at the truth about the gods, never state it.** Don't harp on sailors' stories.
- **It's a glimpse of the world as it sees itself, for players, not a catalogue of the lore.** Every entry should add something new (a custom, a saying, a habit, a place, a bit of history) instead of rehashing the setting. Use broad, confident generalisations; personal anecdotes are rare, and there are no quips.
- **Show, don't tell.** Use a scene, a detail or a quote, and say each thing once.
- **Use plain words with a good rhythm.** For example, "tithes in coin and souls"; no odd, roundabout phrasing.
- **Nothing on the nose.**
- **Follow the register of the creator's reference books** (Dragon Age codex, Pocket Guide to the Empire). Open on a confident claim, describe one custom exactly, use history only where it explains the present, show bias through judgement, write measured full sentences rather than fragments, and close on a pointed line. Aim for about 80–200 words. The creator loved the first entries in this register ("so much better, I can read this") and asked for the whole codex to follow it.
- **Keep a layer between the writer and the setting notes.** Design rules like "orcs are never Vigil" are never said in-world.

**Why:** creator feedback, 2026-11-13. Their analogy: Roman historians recorded battles but never explained hand-to-hand fighting, because everyone knew it. The first codex draft read like a rules document.

**How to apply:** keep the rules in SETTING.md and let the codex assume them. The full codex rules are in breachline/LORE_CODEX.md. Related: [[breachline-project]].

## Magic and industry clarification (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Read [the current setting rules](../../../breachline/SETTING.md#magic) and Ambrel's surviving-university account before briefing new work. Magic can be felt broadly, while skilled use is less common and depends on learning and inherited aptitude. With normal attunement, a small ordinary spell is technically learnable but very difficult for most people; do not turn widespread magical sensitivity into routine spellcasting by ordinary crowds. No fixed percentage of casters is established. Practice develops a connection to a non-physical magical plane; talent can show early. Formal education, apprenticeships, traditions, religious orders and occasional self-teaching all have room. Follow the setting for their scope rather than treating all learning as solitary experimentation. Personal capacity varies greatly; expenditure is exertion and severe overuse can kill. Exhaustion typically progresses from slight loss of control to difficulty concentrating, then physical fatigue, then near-complete loss of control. A highly trained mage can maintain control as they tire, like an athlete keeping form under strain; do not require every exhausted mage to visibly lose control. This does not remove the cost or danger of overexertion. Failed spells usually fizzle or go astray; dangerous unpredictability is possible, especially in someone with substantial raw power and little training. A less magically gifted person is more likely to have a spell simply fizzle. Match a failure's scale to the individual and situation rather than making every lapse spectacular. Training increases capacity and efficiency; food and rest partly replenish energy, and a practitioner can channel external magic from materials. Living beings are generally more efficient at magical conversion than machines. Species comparisons, recovery rates and exact numerical ranges remain tentative; alignment and emotional affinity are still being explored.

Magical energy can power industry through imbued items, mages' charging work and magical stones or materials. Personally owned enchanted items are a significant luxury; do not equip ordinary crowds with routine enchanted possessions. Ordinary people can nevertheless use items such as magical keys or enhanced swords without being spellcasters. Effective use and control of especially powerful items can depend on the wielder's ability; an exceptional staff may exceed a weaker mage's control. Match the demands to the particular item rather than depicting every enchanted possession as difficult to handle. Enchantments also vary in duration and fuel: some persist indefinitely, while others consume energy and need replenishing. A permanently enhanced blade and a staff using stored energy are possible examples, not mandatory rules for those equipment types. Rare places can also retain enduring enchantments for years without an active caster; any particular place and effect remains a proposal until selected. **The Mountain's Lung burns stored mana from stones to provide energy.** Those stones become permanently spent; do not depict them as rechargeable batteries. Direct magic and creature magic remain valid without equipment or universal visible feeds. Portals are possible but generally rare, requiring unusual talent and difficult learned technique.

**Healing and revival:** after death the soul begins to leak away. Almost immediate revival can leave someone mostly intact; later return can leave lasting changes in memory, emotion, independence and bodily function. Soul and body are separate but related, and the body contains some of the soul. Lost soul can be partly regained through healing, with scarring possible. A zombie has a partial soul and a partial body. Neither soul leakage nor scarring has a mandatory visual form.

Vigil would attempt an immediate revival only if the healer were sufficiently strong; it prefers to let the soul rest rather than bring the person back diminished; Genesis wants death to reclaim the body; Fervor probably would not bother with revival. Dominion accepts diminished returns and would generally add magical energy for strength rather than fuller restoration. Gnosis is experimenting: Ambrel might restore people and sustain them with magical energy, a tentative direction rather than routine treatment. Follow the [setting account](../../../breachline/SETTING.md#healing-revival-and-spirits-creator-direction-2026-11-10) for scope and remaining boundaries. Spirits may be conscious but are generally weak, distant or diffuse.

Ambrel concentrates learned and highly developed magical practice because its university survived the Empire's fall. Other places still have mages and tinkerers. This supports uneven capability without making all Ambrel residents casters or all everyday scenes displays of magic. Keep the earlier range from modest useful magic to exceptional large events; rarity does not mean every illustration must be small or quiet.

**Imperial remnants and powerful beings (2026-11-10):** imperial infrastructure survives mostly as ordinary roads, buildings and similar works; functioning mysterious systems are not the default legacy. Dragons are intelligent but primal, with communication requiring a magical connection. Complex ideas can pass through it, but communication is difficult; do not assume effortless ordinary conversation, tame behaviour or the same intelligence for every drake. Dangerous magic is generally treated like other violence, with special enforcement possible for powerful rogue mages; no particular enforcement order is established. Read the corresponding sections in [SETTING.md](../../../breachline/SETTING.md) before extending these ideas.

**Construct behaviour (2026-11-10):** most constructs follow narrow instructions; adaptability is possible but rare. They can have personality without consciousness. The most advanced can be meaningful to humans while remaining distinctly unlike human minds. For future scenes, purposeful habits and responses can suggest character without establishing human-equivalent understanding or personhood. Read the [setting guidance](../../../breachline/SETTING.md#construct-behaviour-creator-direction-2026-11-10); whether any construct is truly conscious remains open.

Apply the following to new card, equipment, character, creature and world art; interface materials use the same painting principles at quieter contrast. Existing approved identities and unrelated review statuses retain their recorded decisions.

CASTING: For new highly aggressive frontline fighters, the vast majority should be men; female soldiers remain occasional. Women remain welcome as mages, archers, civilians and other characters. Preserve specifically approved identities unless the creator requests a change.

FACES: Actively consult the human/species face sheets. Use specific ordinary reference faces for corrective casting, and draw on their varied skulls, noses, jaws, ages and asymmetries when inventing new people. Keep generally appealing faces and occasional exceptional beauty; avoid repeated model faces, compulsory ugliness and uncanny skin detail.

MATERIALS: Skin, wool, wood and stone have naturally diffuse surfaces. Reserve stronger reflections for appropriate metal, glass or water. Avoid waxy faces, uniform surface shine and habitual red cheeks, especially on heavyset women. Keep vibrant color and convincing light.

PAINTED ATTENTION: Match brush size, edge precision and detail locally. Focal features can be precise; secondary people and distant places stay legible with fewer marks and quieter contrast. REFERENCE USE: Accepted references guide anatomy, materials, individual continuity and taste; they do not limit new faces, clothes, kit, settings or magic to a repeating catalog. This review supersedes the first Set 6 prompts and any reading of earlier "consistent resolution" guidance as equal detail everywhere.

- **Painted attention:** the creator welcomes rich, high-fidelity painting, but wants deliberate variation in detail within it. A focal nose, eye or object may be finely described while an ear, sleeve, secondary face or distant building is simplified. Keep backgrounds perceptible. Use edge control, grouped values and varied brush size rather than photographic blur. Several equally sharp people and textures made Muster the levy busy and uncomfortable to look at.

## Style

Keep expressive, believable anatomy with selective facial description. The roaring berserker's face and teeth need simpler treatment at the creator's request. Fine brushwork is welcome elsewhere when it serves the focal idea; pores, etched veins and dense surface noise do not create convincing individuality.

Every image is a traditional oil and acrylic fantasy painting with measured visible brushwork, fine observation and deliberately varied detail. A focal feature may be precise while secondary forms use fewer marks; this is painted attention, not equal definition everywhere. It must never look like a photograph, 3D render, anime, comic or flat vector art. The world is high fantasy.

Concentrate sharp edges and fine detail around the visual idea. Let other edges dissolve into atmosphere, broader strokes or quiet tonal shapes. Painted realism can include smooth translucent passages and selective textured accents; avoid applying the same thick diagonal brush pattern to every surface.

Format: landscape, about 3:2, such as 1536 x 1024. One clear subject should read at small size, with important details kept away from the edges. Include no text, letters, borders, logos, watermarks or signatures.

## Reference-led variety (working interpretation, 2026-11-05)

The creator supplied twelve Magic card images as examples they like and asked for more variety. The observations below are an interpretation to test in Set 2, not individually confirmed preferences. - **Inventive silhouettes:** the construct references (`construct.jpeg`, `Solemn-Simulacrum_MTG-733x1024.png`) use elegant asymmetry, unexpected proportions and negative spaces. Design original creatures and mechanisms from their function. A brass chorister should read as an instrument brought to life, not a human knight with metallic skin.
- **Mood carried by shape and colour:** `creep.jpeg` and `cultist.jpeg` suggest strangeness through long shapes, obscured faces and a few cool tonal masses. Dominion can be patient, pitiful, unsettling or ceremonious without defaulting to gore, fangs, Gothic castles or explosive darkness.

A spell may be an event, a material, a presence or an ambiguous moment; it need not spell out the rules or show every consequence. Secondary affected figures remain an option, not a requirement.
- **Biological invention:** `green.jpg` integrates foliage, growth and creature anatomy into a coherent form. Genesis creatures should have distinctive root, branch, horn or feather silhouettes, rather than all being humanoids dressed in leaves.
- **Scale and breathing room:** `serra.webp` and `sea.jpeg` let wings, monumental shapes and surrounding space establish scale. Large subjects need not fill every edge; ordinary figures can be small within a selective setting. This does not authorize adding wings, cities or monumental power when absent from the brief.
- **Ordinary humans with lived gestures:** `human 2.jpeg`, `human 3.jpeg` and `human.jpeg` use practical equipment, asymmetry, weight shifts and group rhythm. Show the action's intention and a specific expression. Supporting figures remain subordinate; only rarely aim an attack at the viewer.
- **A wider painted range:** retain believable materials and painted realism, while allowing soft atmospheric glazing, crisp contours at the focal point, restrained expressive distortion in fantastical anatomy and varied brush scale. Do not copy the first batch's identical surface texture, warm ochre cast or close-up hero framing.
- **Distinct colour scripts:** choose a limited, scene-specific palette and one lighting idea. Luminous cyan, cold violet, deep forest green, dry sunlit ochre and quiet neutral daylight can coexist in one set. Alignment identity should survive without putting its emblem on every object.

Across a batch, check variety in camera distance, facing direction, body gesture, silhouette, mood, background structure and dominant colour.

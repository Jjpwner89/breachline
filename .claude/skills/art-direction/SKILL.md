The creator's latest feedback and the approved art direction take precedence over all art briefs, including their suggested poses, facing directions, framing, compositions and rendering. ## Magic and industry clarification (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Read [the current setting rules](../../../breachline/SETTING.md#magic) and Ambrel's surviving-university account before briefing new work. Magic can be felt broadly, while skilled use is less common and depends on learning and inherited aptitude. Personal capacity varies greatly; expenditure is exertion and severe overuse can kill. Species comparisons, recovery, training effects and exact numerical ranges remain tentative.

Magical energy can power industry through imbued items, mages' charging work and magical stones or materials. **The Mountain's Lung burns stored mana from stones to provide energy.** Do not assume its stones' afterlife or rechargeability. Direct magic and creature magic remain valid without equipment or universal visible feeds. Portals are possible but generally rare. Healing is powerful but limited; possible costly revival follows faction morality, with Dominion willing to accept a diminished return. Spirits may be conscious but are generally weak, distant or diffuse.

Ambrel concentrates learned and highly developed magical practice because its university survived the Empire's fall. Other places still have mages and tinkerers. This supports uneven capability without making all Ambrel residents casters or all everyday scenes displays of magic. Keep the earlier range from modest useful magic to exceptional large events; rarity does not mean every illustration must be small or quiet.

The creator requested a new concept-art batch informed by these rules, alongside questions about the remaining gaps. New paintings are studies awaiting review, not additions to the twelve-card handoff or automatic canon.

The creator accepted all seventeen round-07 revisions and asked for these updates throughout. Apply the following to new card, equipment, character, creature and world art; interface materials use the same painting principles at quieter contrast. Existing approved identities and unrelated review statuses retain their recorded decisions.

CASTING: For new highly aggressive frontline fighters, the vast majority should be men; female soldiers remain occasional. Women remain welcome as mages, archers, civilians and other characters. Preserve specifically approved identities unless the creator requests a change.

FACES: Actively consult the human/species face sheets. Use specific ordinary reference faces for corrective casting, and draw on their varied skulls, noses, jaws, ages and asymmetries when inventing new people. Keep generally appealing faces and occasional exceptional beauty; avoid repeated model faces, compulsory ugliness and uncanny skin detail.

MATERIALS: Skin, wool, wood and stone have naturally diffuse surfaces. Reserve stronger reflections for appropriate metal, glass or water. Avoid waxy faces, uniform surface shine and habitual red cheeks, especially on heavyset women. Keep vibrant color and convincing light.

PAINTED ATTENTION: Match brush size, edge precision and detail locally. Focal features can be precise; secondary people and distant places stay legible with fewer marks and quieter contrast. Do not combine coarse texture with uniformly hairline detail, render everything equally sharply, or substitute camera blur and tunnel focus.

DESIGN RANGE: Constructs may have abstract functional anatomy, asymmetry and open space; they need not resemble armored human knights. Gnosis combines scholarly high fantasy with broader steam-era craft, not an optics uniform. Blend selected Asian armor details into the established kit; modest curved eaves are a valid architectural option. These are examples, not mandatory formulas.

MAGIC AND PRESENTATION: Direct personal and creature magic, imbued objects and industrial applications can coexist without universal equipment, visible power feeds or glowing threads. Keep grounded steam machinery and creature/direct magical flight distinct from the rejected hovering magitech vessels. Choose a compelling image; card rules do not require an ability demonstration.

REFERENCE USE: Accepted references guide anatomy, materials, individual continuity and taste; they do not limit new faces, clothes, kit, settings or magic to a repeating catalog. Preserve accepted content and vary presentation. Feedback applies at the image or batch scope given unless the creator makes it general.

## Visual appeal comes first (creator correction, 2026-11-09)

The goal of a card illustration is an image the creator loves that belongs in this world. Judge it by presentation, imaginative design, light, colour, silhouette, material, atmosphere and character. Appeal can be quiet, severe, unsettling, beautiful or mysterious; it does not mean only exciting or fun.

Card rules are design metadata, not a composition brief. Keep the recognizable subject and established world, but do not make a unit demonstrate its abilities or illustrate every keyword. A scholar can be compelling without zapping; a figure with Guard need not protect somebody; an enchanted material need not be shown being applied. An armoured angel flying in the sky is the creator's example of a strong presentation that needs no explanation of its rules.

- Begin each new brief with the visual appeal being pursued and the relevant accepted references. Choose staging for that appeal. Keep costs, keywords, numerical effects and tactical outcomes out of image-generation prompts; they may remain separately in card-design records.
- Preserve what was attractive about a reference, beyond its equipment inventory: proportions, attitude, restraint, material character and the particular magical idea. The wardwright's small orbiting stones are more interesting to the creator than the later combat explosion.
- Purposeful activity remains a positive option, not an obligation to invent a task, victim, bystander, application sequence or before-and-after demonstration. A portrait, presence, material study or creature in its habitat can be sufficient. Movement, combat and dramatic light remain available when they improve the painting.
- Check a proposed image without its title or rules: what makes it worth looking at? If a prop or secondary figure exists mainly to explain an ability, remove it or find a stronger visual reason for it.
- Do not overcorrect into uniformly static, subdued or nonmagical pictures. Preserve the range already liked: the lancer's presence, the bone harvester's activity and colour, the court informant's gesture and divided light, the great landscapes and the inventive creatures. Specific card feedback remains specific. Brightlance's off-screen hit does not revoke the general permission for occasional secondary enemies elsewhere.

## Applying this direction to new cards

- Use the latest creator feedback and current briefs; original generation prompts and discarded attempts are historical records. A card-specific choice stays specific to that card.
- Check connected anatomy and physical contact before selecting an image: trace hips through knees and ankles to feet, check the number and connection of limbs and fingers, and make grips, support, shields and object overlaps physically coherent. Judge the whole pose, not isolated body parts.
- Keep scale consistent through familiar objects and a shared ground plane. Helmets, weapons and secondary people must support the subject's size rather than contradict it.
- Assess painted style across the whole scene: staging, lighting, palette, edges and detail should belong together. A face-only retouch does not fix an animated-film appearance caused by the full composition.
- For a requested local edit, preserve the approved pose and unaffected parts. Specify anatomical left/right together with screen position and the hand's role. Fit a new prop to the existing pose, and inspect both hands and legs again afterward. If the creator rejects the underlying composition or concept, replace it rather than accumulating local repairs.

## Style

Keep expressive, believable anatomy with selective facial description. The roaring berserker's face and teeth need simpler treatment at the creator's request. Fine brushwork is welcome elsewhere when it serves the focal idea; pores, etched veins and dense surface noise do not create convincing individuality.

Every image is a traditional oil and acrylic fantasy painting with measured visible brushwork, fine observation and deliberately varied detail. A focal feature may be precise while secondary forms use fewer marks; this is painted attention, not equal definition everywhere. It must never look like a photograph, 3D render, anime, comic or flat vector art. The world is high fantasy.

Concentrate sharp edges and fine detail around the visual idea. Let other edges dissolve into atmosphere, broader strokes or quiet tonal shapes. Painted realism can include smooth translucent passages and selective textured accents; avoid applying the same thick diagonal brush pattern to every surface.

Format: landscape, about 3:2, such as 1536 x 1024. One clear subject should read at small size, with important details kept away from the edges. Include no text, letters, borders, logos, watermarks or signatures.

# Animal feedback map

This records the trajectory of the creature collaboration through 13 November 2026. It is supporting design memory, not a second selection database. The [reference guide](../../../../breachline/ANIMAL_REFERENCE_HANDOFF.txt) and [joined index](../../../../breachline/art/accepted-animals-2026-11-13/animal-reference-index.json) determine current sources and status. Read only the relevant round below; do not revive work merely because it once had a follow-up request.

## First lineups and selected reference sheets

The initial choices were Lampkeeper CL2–5, Moltwalker CL1–2, Ploughback CL3–2's body with CL3–3's scales, and Hooktail CL4–4 with an entirely furred tail. RF1–RF4 were subsequently confirmed as very good and should be preserved.

Shieldhound's normal fur and dog-like body were too literal, and its shield did not fit the anatomy. This called for integrated skull/neck/shoulder construction and a different covering for that animal, not a ban on fur across the collection. CL6–1, the sieve-muzzle grazer, was liked with a smaller, less extreme mouth; the creator explicitly confirmed the identity. Those revisions have their own unresolved status in the guide.

**Lesson:** useful strange anatomy must belong to the animal. Local corrections stay local; liked animals do not need to be redesigned to satisfy the next exploration.

## Body plans, faces and the mistaken head-replacement round

After RF1–RF4, exploration was too close to known Earth animals. The creator asked for wider body plans and a range of skin, fur, feathers and scales. Weight and movement were getting closer.

Loomstep BP1–2 was especially liked. Foldstride BP1–1, Shelfback BP1–3 and Needlehip BP1–5 also worked; Needlehip's lizard-like face, fur and whip tail exemplified an interesting combination. BP1–4 Siltdrum was rejected as fat and ugly: that specific reaction does not prohibit heavy-bodied animals. Ribhide BP2–4, Scaleskein BP2–5 and Gullied swimmer BP3–4 were retained; Purlrunner began as a tentative interest.

The creator noticed too many lizard heads and suggested mammalian and other shapes, while explicitly allowing snake/reptile faces. The assistant changed heads on existing picks. The correction was to keep the originals for HV1–HV6 and HV8, choose HV7–1 for Purlrunner, and apply broader head variety to NEW creatures. Faces still had to make sense.

NC1–2, NC1–3, NC1–5, NC1–6 and NC2–4 were subsequently selected.

**Lesson:** preserve accepted visual identity; explore variety in new generations, across whole animals rather than detachable heads.

## Larger wildlife and edge experiments

LG1–4 Vaultstalker was strongly liked as a larger honey-badger impression with scales. LG1–5 Pithbreaker and LG1–6 Mantlebrood also worked. Recognisable animal corollaries plus clear unfamiliar anatomy were successful; this coexists with the desire for more original body plans. Pithbreaker should read larger than a small armadillo; older oversized prompt language was not a settled measurement.

Throat combs and filter-like jaw frills were overused and disliked. LG1 being “decent” did not individually select all six takes. LG2–4 Barkdrummer's head plate and feather-scale combination worked, with overlap noted against another design. LG2–1 Pollenferry's leathery skin transitioning to feather/fur was liked.

LG2–6 Scalelease was rejected because bugs beneath its scales did not make sense. This is not a ban on symbiosis. LG2–5 Sporewarden was somewhat gross but appealing; remove fungus while keeping the animal. The creator requested a new name; **Coilneck** is an assistant-proposed working name, not a final creator choice. Revised sheets retain their own review status.

**Lesson:** appeal can survive some awkwardness or grossness. Keep purposeful anatomy and material transitions; stop repeating one novelty. Species overlap is a reason to distinguish roles, not to merge identities.

Sources: [larger studies](../../../../breachline/art/work-in-progress/larger-creatures-2026-11-13/), [later reference sheets](../../../../breachline/art/work-in-progress/larger-creature-references-2026-11-13/).

## Ecological gaps and independent A/B requests

The creator wanted closer ecological inspiration from Scavengers Reign and invited creative edge experiments. Six gaps were explored with separate described and freeform requests: dung/seed burial, understone microbial-film grazing, plant-grown nurseries, brood parasitism, aquatic-linked parasite life cycles, and social colony economies. The freeform prompt had to remain independent of the proposed anatomy.

Selected outcomes and later refinements:
- E1–B became ER1, keeping the unusual burrower with its back sack removed.
- Both E2 grazers were rejected.
- Both plant-nursery animals were liked. E3–B became ER2 Gall glider, able to glide on its small membranes.
- E4–A became ER3 Cradle thief with a slightly changed fur pattern. E4–B became ER4, an independent bird; its brood-parasite framing was removed.
- E5–A became ER5 Ford-thread: larger aquatic body, smaller swimmer's rounded face, explicitly confirmed.
- E5–B's translucent parasite was liked. The scale-backed, finned shore hunter shown with it became the separate ER6; never make it a transformation or later life stage of the parasite.
- E6–A's ant-like workers, warriors and internal nuts were liked. E6–B became a small herd with varied responsibilities, not a hive. Its inaccessible back pouch led to an underslung/no-pouch comparison; ER7–1's accessible chest pouch was chosen.

ER1–ER6 and ER7–1 were approved. The later world review approved the selected ecology groupings and clarified **Crested bird as predatory and mobile**. Exact unknown mechanisms remain open.

**Lesson:** ecology can produce excellent form variety when the freeform request is genuinely free. Make storage reachable, movement usable and relationships intelligible; don't infer a life cycle from incidental co-presence.

Sources: [ecological pairs](../../../../breachline/art/work-in-progress/ecological-niche-pairs-2026-11-13/), [refinements](../../../../breachline/art/work-in-progress/great-animals-and-ecology-refinements-2026-11-13/).

## Large, extra-large and massive review

G1–1 Cragwool was liked with a moderately more predatory, less stub-nosed face. G1–2 Reedstrider was liked with its copper mohawk-like fibres extended over the body. These were local edits. G1–3/4/5/6 were cut, and one replacement large animal was requested. FR1, FR2 and LN1 remain separately unreviewed.

Extra-large G2–3 Gravelmouth, G2–4 Woodland breaker and G2–5 Salt-flat migrant were kept; G2–1/2/6 were rejected. The long-legged migrant's sense of travel was liked.

G3–2 Undertow could continue with shorter, broader, continuous side fins and flatter edges, preserving its small rear triangle. The other G3 options were rejected. New giants needed more imaginative distributions of mass and movement; the creator specifically warned against carrying small-animal restrictions over to them, citing Scavengers Reign and Kenshi.

The closing review selected MG6 Fanback, which is exactly MN2–1, and preferred FR3 Undertow. This batch was too scary. MG4 Deepkeel was called weird-looking; it and the other giant experiments were set aside. This was not an explicit rejection of every unselected giant and did not request another generation round.

**Lesson:** give giant designs their own physical architecture and sense of scale; avoid defaulting to threatening mouths or horror. Fanback's growths/fan mechanism and Undertow's precise diet are not settled just because the visual worked.

## Adoption into the world and future use

The creator approved the ecology selections and asked to connect the animals to future cards, especially Genesis, and other art. Consult the existing collection first; consider its grazers, predators, scavengers and other residents in fields, around farmers, in waterways and in background scenes. Preserve these world inhabitants while allowing new species where useful.

The reference guide is more important than this skill. The skill collects reusable design lessons so future exploration can continue without forgetting the journey. It does not reopen the finished giant round, replace selected references, automatically approve pending studies, or assign card rules.

For older detail not reproduced here, use [the general art history](../../art-direction/HISTORY.md) and the linked source-round records. Original giant-sea-creature/Tidevault scale corrections remain local to those concepts; they are not a ceiling on all future massive wildlife.

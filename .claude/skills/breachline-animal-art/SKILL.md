# Breachline animal art

The **reference guide is primary**. This skill is the supporting design memory for making more animals. The selected animals already inhabit the world; preserve them and build outward from them.

## Start with the animals that exist

Read [ANIMAL_REFERENCE_HANDOFF.txt](../../../breachline/ANIMAL_REFERENCE_HANDOFF.txt) first. For a particular animal or niche, consult the [joined reference index](../../../breachline/art/accepted-animals-2026-11-13/animal-reference-index.json) and [ecology gallery](../../../breachline/art/accepted-animals-2026-11-13/ecologies.html). They hold exact selected images, crops, identities, ecological roles and open details. Read the [current general art direction](../art-direction/SKILL.md) for painting, presentation and image-generation conventions, and [SETTING.md](../../../breachline/SETTING.md) for world facts.

These are project-relative links. Resolve this folder's real filesystem location first if the skill was loaded through the Codex discovery symlink.

- The creator's latest instructions take precedence. The reference guide/index govern established animal identities and selections; this skill governs future exploration, not revisions to those choices.
- The selected collection and its ecology groupings are approved for world use. Keep explicitly unresolved details open. An older “visual only” note does not undo the later world/ecology acceptance.
- Search existing roles before inventing an animal for a card or scene. If the user expressly asks for new animals, use the collection to find variety and gaps, then create new proposals.
- Look here for a field grazer, an animal around a farmer, life in a canal or a forest background—not only a creature starring on its own card. Genesis is a useful starting point, not an exclusive alignment for wildlife. Nearby wildlife does not automatically become livestock.
- Existing animals need new poses, individuals and situations, not redesigned heads or bodies. New general feedback applies to new work unless the creator asks to revise a selected animal.

## The design target

Make original wildlife with convincing weight, movement, feeding and ecological presence. The creator wants the world to feel populated by lives that make sense together.

The useful inspiration from **Scavengers Reign** is ecological inventiveness, tactile surfaces, interdependence and surprising but integrated life. **Kenshi** informed the unusual proportions and imposing presence of big animals. Translate those qualities into original design language in prompts; avoid named franchise references or copied creatures. Keep the project's painted fantasy treatment.

A familiar animal or niche can be a starting point. Successful examples can evoke a honey badger, anteater, armadillo or kangaroo while differing clearly in anatomy. Later feedback also asks for bodies that cannot be reduced to a familiar Earth species with a new coat. The earlier “one or two twists” heuristic is a starting aid, not a universal limit—especially for giants.

Allow quiet, awkward, beautiful, eerie, forceful and occasionally mildly gross animals. Don't equate imagination with ugliness, threat or a pile of ornaments. Most animals simply live: grazing, hunting, scavenging, burrowing or reproducing. They need not all help people, wield magic or carry symbionts. Ordinary Earth-like wildlife can also exist; exploration should add something distinctive.

## What to vary, and what must still work

**Whole bodies and movement.** Vary the distribution of mass, limb arrangements, support points, torso depth, head placement, necks, balance, locomotion and silhouette. Show how the animal stands, turns or feeds. Trace limb connections and count them; make ground contact and scale credible. Avoid giving every take the same quadruped skeleton or the same exotic feature.

**Integrated heads.** Vary skull mass, muzzle length, jaws, nose, eye placement and ears, including mammalian and other forms. Reptilian and snake-like heads remain welcome within that mix. A face must belong to its neck, body, feeding action and covering. The creator's head-variety note meant NEW generations, not interchangeable heads grafted onto liked designs.

**Covering and material.** Explore leathery skin, fur, feathers and scales, and convincing transitions among them. Let coverings affect silhouette, flexibility and the animal's physical character. Needlehip's reptilian face, fur and whip tail, Pollenferry's leather-to-feather/fur transition, and Barkdrummer's head plate and feather-scale combination are successful examples, not batch templates.

**Purposeful structures.** Shields, pouches, membranes and feeding organs must attach and function plausibly. An animal must be able to load or use its own food storage. A shield should grow into the anatomy; a growth or symbiont needs an intelligible relationship. Do not use jaw frills or filter-like throat combs as the recurring novelty: the creator found them overused. This is not a ban on every fringe, filter feeder or symbiosis.

**Variety across a group.** Change more than colour and faces. Compare mass, stance, coverings, head type, feeding role, scale and mood across the lineup. Avoid a procession of lizard faces, dorsal sacks, plant hybrids, fliers or one liked animal's signature feature. Include restrained takes and some experiments around the edges of the request when exploring. No fixed quota or mandatory novelty applies to every batch.

## Scale has its own design space

For large, extra-large and massive animals, do not over-apply notes aimed at small creatures. Reconsider body architecture, support and movement at that scale, rather than enlarging the same small skeleton. Use a clear comparison or environment when size matters, without inventing precise dimensions the creator has not set.

Truly enormous animals are rare and awe-inspiring. The closing giant round became too scary: future giant work should preserve unfamiliar presence without making horror or threatening mouths the default. This does not erase accepted predators or prohibit danger. Fanback and the preferred Undertow revision are selected; their exact references are in the guide. The other closing giant studies are set aside, not automatically rejected or an outstanding generation task.

## Explore through ecology

Use the approved ecological roles as design anchors. Study habitat, food, movement and relationships; avoid forcing an animal to visibly demonstrate card mechanics. Look across the full study audit when investigating a gap, while distinguishing an untouched niche from a niche with rejected attempts and from a niche with an accepted inhabitant.

The successful paired-niche round used two genuinely independent approaches. When that comparison is requested or useful:
- **A, described:** supply the proposed anatomy, niche and intended behaviour.
- **B, freeform:** supply the same niche and current world/art goals, leaving anatomy, name and palette open. Do not show it A's design, image or output.
- Keep presentation comparable and preserve exact prompts. Do not turn this successful experiment into a compulsory format for every future request.

Keep animal identities distinct. Similar jobs need not mean the same species; an animal pictured alongside a parasite is not automatically its later life stage. A small herd with different responsibilities is not necessarily a hive. New mechanisms and species relationships remain proposals until chosen.

## Make the next review useful

For genuinely new species, normally follow the established **lineup → selected reference → scene** workflow, with about six varied takes per lineup when appropriate. Honor a requested alternative such as paired niche plates or a direct edit. Existing selected animals can proceed to new scenes.

Each concept needs a clear code and a short two-to-three-sentence description: what is shown, its proposed place in the world, and any material uncertainty. Give local changes narrowly; preserve the liked source's body, face, covering and other untouched features.

Stage new art under `breachline/art/work-in-progress/`. Record exact prompts, source images, selected crop/cell and review decisions; use the available image-generation tool and its current instructions for actual images. Before selecting art references, check `breachline/art/art-review/selection-guide.json` for exclusions.

For animal review galleries, preserve shared source sheets and label choices clearly. The standing request is to cover rejected designs with Xs; hidden unselected alternatives must retain their actual status. Rejected, unselected, set aside, pending and accepted are different decisions. A sheet containing one liked cell is not wholly approved.

When the creator selects a new animal, preserve its exact reference in the library and update the animal guide/index and ecology records. Extend world facts only to the scope accepted. Do not turn selection into automatic card statistics or roster implementation.

## Keep feedback useful

Read [the feedback map](references/feedback-map.md) when interpreting earlier rounds, local exceptions or superseded directions. The reference guide remains the operational home for exact animal continuity; the map preserves why the design approach changed.

Add reusable lessons to this skill at the scope actually given. Put animal-specific choices into the guide/index and source review, and preserve relevant history in the feedback map or general art history. Update superseded guidance rather than accumulating contradictory prohibitions. Link new records instead of copying the full roster here.

These briefs propose five painted campaign maps, one for each alignment. The game draws its route markers and labels on top of each painting, so the paintings must contain no interface elements and must leave room for them.

## Status of what's here

- **Vigil** matches the campaign already in the game. Its route and places are drafts awaiting the creator's verdicts.
- **Gnosis, Dominion, Fervor and Genesis** don't exist in the game yet. Their routes, working titles and stops are proposals made for this brief.
- **Nothing here is canon.** No place, event or enemy in these maps joins the setting until the creator approves it.
- **Separate regions (creator's decision, 2026-11-06):** each campaign gets its own region of the same world, rather than one shared region. This answers the question raised by the River Road study.

## Requirements for every map

### Format

- 1536 x 1024, landscape, 3:2, PNG.
- Full-bleed painting with no frame, parchment edge or tabletop.
- A high three-quarter bird's-eye view. Mountains and buildings show some height, and little or no sky shows. Keep the view steep enough that the route reads as a map, not as a landscape painting.

### How it fits the game

Each stop has a coordinate, given as a percentage across and down the image from the top left. The game draws a numbered round seal centred on that point, about 6% of the image width, with the stop's name just below it.

- **Landmark:** paint it immediately above the coordinate, so the seal sits at its foot and doesn't cover it.
- **Calm ground:** keep a quiet area of low detail centred on the coordinate, about 8% wide and 12% tall, so the seal and label stay legible. Avoid very dark or very bright patches there.
- **Road:** paint one continuous physical road, track or trail from the start point through all six coordinates in order. It should look like a real worn route, not a game path. It can narrow, fade under trees, cross water or be overgrown where the brief says so, but it must remain followable.
- **Accuracy:** stay within about 3% of each coordinate. The game's coordinates can be adjusted slightly to the finished painting, but the order and overall shape must hold.
- **Edges:** keep landmarks and stops at least 5% from every edge. The road may leave the frame at its start point.
- **Small sizes:** the map appears about 800 pixels wide on a laptop and 340 on a phone. Each landmark needs a distinct silhouette that still reads at phone size, and quiet space must separate the stops.

### Do not paint

- Text, letters, numbers, labels, map symbols or a compass
- Pins, seals, dots, arrows, dashed lines, glowing routes or highlighted paths
- Borders, political colouring or an emblem on every building
- Fighting, gore, corpses or anything aimed at the viewer
- Glowing gold scars or threads, or anything explaining the Cataclysm or the fate of the gods
- Opaline Kin, who are explicitly excluded from the lore
- Stonehorn people, whose design is unresolved
- Enormous creatures, except the single distant Dawnsail in the Gnosis brief

### Painting and variety

- **Medium:** traditional oil and acrylic fantasy painting with visible, confident brushwork. Never a photograph, 3D render, satellite view, vector map, anime or game screenshot.
- **Detail:** concentrate crisp detail on the six landmarks, and let the land between them fall into broader strokes. Don't cover every surface with the same dense texture or diagonal brush pattern.
- **Economy:** use a few large value shapes. Simple, well-placed miniature figures work better than crowds of tiny detail.
- **People:** where they appear, make them tiny and busy with something specific. A tiny traveller on the road gives scale.
- **Across the set:** each map needs its own climate, palette, light, terrain and route shape. They should not look like one template recoloured. Watch for the repetition already noted in the art direction: streaky skies, ivy on old stone, rain and grey haze.
- **Alignment:** let identity come from land, materials, light and activity, not from emblems.

## The maps

### 1. Vigil: The Shrine Road

**File:** `map-vigil.png`
**Region:** the western river basins, climbing to the foot of the central mountains.
**Palette and light:** gold, ivory and sky blue, in clear warm morning light across green-gold land. Avoid red cloth.
**Idea:** a long climb from ordinary country to a very small, uncertain holy place. The modest shrine should matter more than any grand architecture. It's the emotional point of the map.

**Road starts:** bottom left, at about (4, 95).

| # | Stop | Across, down | Landmark above the point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The ford | 16, 74 | A broad shallow ford where the road crosses a river that runs from top to bottom of the left side. A few russet raider tents and picketed animals on the near bank. |
| 2 | The closed road | 41, 83 | A dark, deliberately overgrown forest. Old paving disappears under trees and thicket, with a few felled trunks across it. The forest reads as guarded, not simply wild. |
| 3 | The pass | 56, 53 | A narrow pass between two distinct rocky shoulders, with a rough cairn and a watch fire at the top. Mountains begin here and rise toward the upper right. |
| 4 | The lower gate | 31, 34 | A lone old imperial gate, weathered and larger than anything around it, standing with no walls. Tiny scholars' lamps and surveying instruments are set out around it. |
| 5 | The fallow field | 61, 23 | One large pale field of untouched grass on a high shelf, with low rounded mounds and no furrows. Unsettling because it's so quiet. No bones or figures. |
| 6 | The shrine steps | 86, 17 | A long worn stair up a bare height to one small stone room with a single doorway. It should look almost too small for the journey. No glow and no visible lamp flame. |

### 2. Gnosis: The Long Survey (working title)

**File:** `map-gnosis.png`
**Region:** the eastern coast and the country behind it.
**Palette and light:** deep blue, teal, brass and amber at dusk, with lamplight beginning to show in the settled places. Dry evening, no rain.
**Idea:** human ingenuity spreading out from a city into old country: canals, aqueducts and instruments, ending at a place built by the Empire that its makers no longer understand.

**Road starts:** right edge, in the lower part of the harbour, at about (97, 86).

| # | Stop | Across, down | Landmark above the point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ambrel | 86, 76 | A compact harbour city of slate roofs and canals, with a brass-topped lamp tower as its silhouette. Sea to the right. |
| 2 | The waterworks | 62, 62 | Sluices and a stepped reservoir feeding a long aqueduct, with tiny workers and a small construct at a repair. Useful, not grim. |
| 3 | The hill farms | 38, 74 | Terraced farms and scattered houses, with new irrigation channels and a tiny argument of carts at a junction. The ordinary people affected by progress. |
| 4 | The checkpoint | 17, 52 | A pale, disciplined roadside fort in Vigil's ivory and gold, set across the road where it enters high country. Scenery only: a suspicious neighbour, not a city. |
| 5 | The dry canal | 40, 30 | A huge empty Old Empire canal cut straight through the land, with a broken lock gate and tiny surveyors in its bed. Its scale hints at lost ability. |
| 6 | The silent observatory | 16, 15 | A plateau-top observatory with a great open dome and a long instrument, unlit and pointing at nothing visible. Amber lamps of a small camp beside it. No beam of light. |

**One rare sight:** a single Dawnsail far out over the sea at the upper right, small with distance, around (78, 22). Use `breachline/art/concepts/dawnsail-passage.png` for its anatomy. Leave it out if it distracts from the route.

### 3. Dominion: The Quiet Order (working title)

**File:** `map-dominion.png`
**Region:** the cold northern rim.
**Palette and light:** ash grey, bone, tarnished silver and deep violet, under even, cold overcast light, with heather and lichen for colour. Keep it beautiful and readable, not murky. Avoid Gothic castles, fangs, gore and explosive darkness.
**Idea:** order closing in. The road spirals inward through land that grows emptier, tidier and more still, toward a black lake at the centre. Dread comes from neatness and patience.

**Road starts:** bottom left, at about (3, 94).

| # | Stop | Across, down | Landmark above the point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The tithe village | 12, 80 | A small living village with smoke from its chimneys, where carts stand in an orderly line beside a pale counting house. |
| 2 | The barrows | 50, 88 | A long field of rounded barrows in rows, with tiny figures carrying something carefully between them. No bones in view. |
| 3 | The salt causeway | 87, 68 | A straight raised causeway across a pale salt marsh, lined with evenly spaced posts. |
| 4 | The bone orchard | 78, 24 | Rows of bare white trees, carefully pruned, with a single tiny gardener at work. Whether they're trees at all should stay uncertain. |
| 5 | The silent town | 34, 18 | A neat town with clean streets, shuttered windows and no smoke at all, in contrast with stop 1. |
| 6 | The court of still water | 50, 50 | A low, wide, severe palace of pale stone on an island in a perfectly still black lake at the centre of the map, reached by one narrow bridge. |

### 4. Fervor: No Throne (working title)

**File:** `map-fervor.png`
**Region:** the dry southern interior and its red foothills.
**Palette and light:** ember orange, iron, soot and dry ochre, in low late sun with long shadows and small fires across the land. Hot and open, unlike the other maps. Keep it free of lava and apocalyptic skies.
**Idea:** free bands on the move across a huge open country, riding toward a claimant's seat of power to break it. The map should feel loud, restless and alive, with many small camps and trails.

**Road starts:** top left, at about (3, 6).

| # | Stop | Across, down | Landmark above the point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The moot | 14, 22 | A ring of russet tents and banners around a bare gathering ground, with tiny crowds and one small fire. A vote, not a parade. |
| 2 | The moving market | 44, 15 | A long line of wagons, pack animals and awnings drawn up across a dry river crossing, as if it could leave by morning. |
| 3 | The salt pan | 76, 27 | A blinding pale salt flat with a small working camp at its edge and tiny loaded carts. The brightest shape on the map, away from the stop's calm ground. |
| 4 | The red gorge | 52, 50 | A deep, winding gorge of red rock with a rope bridge, seen from above, and a tiny giant carrying something heavy across the far rim. |
| 5 | The toll wall | 22, 62 | A new kingdom's stone toll station closing a gap in the hills, with an orderly line of carts. Everything the bands dislike, seen at a small scale. |
| 6 | The high seat | 70, 80 | A stepped hill with an ancient stone seat at the very top, open to the sky, with a fresh ring of tents and new scaffolding around its base. A newcomer is trying to make it a throne again. |

### 5. Genesis: The Green Return (working title)

**File:** `map-genesis.png`
**Region:** the deep eastern forests, following one great river.
**Palette and light:** moss, bark, deep green and amber, in dappled light breaking through moving cloud, so pools of sun fall across the forest. Green should vary widely, with no flat mass.
**Idea:** one great river runs from upper right to lower left, and the road follows it. Nature is alive and busy at the start. Industry interrupts it, and at the end the wild is already reclaiming an old imperial city.

**Road starts:** top right, at about (97, 6).

| # | Stop | Across, down | Landmark above the point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The mother grove | 84, 16 | Very old trees with distinct silhouettes around a clear spring, with a tiny alderkin and a person planting together. |
| 2 | The reedways | 58, 24 | A wide fen threaded with channels and hidden reed paths, with tiny flat boats and a reedcrown or two grazing in the shallows. |
| 3 | The herd's crossing | 70, 52 | A broad grass opening where a herd of large beasts fords the river. Big animals, not colossal: their size should feel impressive beside the tiny people watching. |
| 4 | The cut line | 42, 46 | A hard straight strip of stumps through the forest, with a logging camp, stacked timber and a new road. The one place on the map with straight lines. |
| 5 | The dam | 48, 76 | A new brass-and-stone dam across the river, with a still reservoir behind it and a thin trickle below. Striking, well made and clearly out of place. |
| 6 | The green city | 16, 72 | A huge Old Empire city in the lower left, with its great stone halls split by trees and vines, and roofless rooms full of green. Not flooded and not on the sea. Keep overgrowth varied rather than ivy on stone. |

## Deliver

1. Save the five PNGs to `breachline/art/maps/`, using the file names above.
2. Save the final prompt for each in `breachline/art/maps/manifest.json`, with its file name, title, stop list and a note that its contents are proposals.
3. Before finishing, check each map at phone width: can you follow the road from stop to stop, and tell the six landmarks apart? Regenerate any that fail.

Don't change game code or existing art, and don't run `art/build-web.sh`: it's sized for cards, and would shrink these maps too far. Connecting the finished maps to the game is a separate step.

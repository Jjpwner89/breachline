# Breachline card art briefs: set 2

This file holds art briefs for 29 new images: 27 cards and 2 lands. Card names may still change.

## Instructions for the image generator

1. Read `.claude/skills/art-direction/SKILL.md` first. The creator's latest feedback and approved art direction OVERRIDE these briefs, including poses, facing, framing and composition. Use the brief as a starting point; redesign conflicting presentation rather than reverting to an older direction.
2. Generate one image per card using the current approved direction, the style block below and the card's subject. Preserve subject, gameplay idea and established lore unless the creator changes them. Refresh old prompts to incorporate the latest feedback before generating.
3. Make each image a landscape PNG at about 3:2, for example 1536 x 1024.
4. Save each image in this `art/` folder under the file name given in its heading.
5. Skip any card whose image file already exists, so a run can be resumed or a single card regenerated by deleting its file.
6. Use the approved concepts `concepts/the-still-flame.png`, `concepts/light-without-an-answer.png` and `concepts/across-the-silence.png` for painted treatment, lighting and scene integration, as appropriate. `sunmane-lancer.png` remains a material-quality reference. Do not copy any reference pose, palette, framing or subject by default.
7. Change only the files in this `art/` folder.
8. If you can't generate images in this environment, stop and say so. Don't substitute placeholders, drawings made in code, or images from the web.

When finished, list the files you created and any cards you couldn't complete.

## Confirmed direction from the concept-art review

Use the current art-direction skill and the approved concept paintings in `concepts/` when interpreting these briefs. The creator especially values:

- Story and image reinforce one another. Across the Silence is a strong positive reference because its figure, gold thread, distance and light make the gods and summoner connection visible.
- Grand landscapes can include a small character to establish scale, point of view and emotional stakes. A figure need not fill the frame.
- Some abstraction is welcome, especially for mystery, metaphysical ideas or enormous scale. Use it occasionally rather than making it the default for every image.
- Lighting should organize the scene and link its subjects. The Still Flame's dynamic warm light, visible brush strokes, integrated characters and small opening of blue sky are specifically loved.
- Absorbed, purposeful activity is highly effective. In Light Without an Answer, the inventor's involvement in the lighting experiment and her small construct companion make the scene compelling.
- Inventive creatures and constructs should feel like participants in the world. The little helper's design and role are strongly liked; preserve that functional imagination without repeating its exact anatomy or every scene's workshop.
- Alignment identity can be clear without being heavy-handed. Light Without an Answer reads as Gnosis/blue through its activity, materials, light and palette without relying on all-blue colouring or repeated emblems.
- Keep the economical, visible painted treatment of these approved concepts. Use meaningful selected detail, natural faces and varied brush scale; do not return to uncanny facial or surface microdetail.

Apply these principles within each card's subject and explicit constraints. A creator override for an individual card takes precedence. The creature/people concept records are in `concepts/CONCEPT_BRIEFS_CREATURES_2026-11-05.json`; consult the catalogue in `../SETTING.md` for current inclusion and proposal status. Opaline Kin are excluded from lore and cards: their painting is a style reference only. These explorations do not change this card list.

Latest scale feedback (2026-11-05): truly immense animals should feel rare and inspire awe; do not make every beast gigantic. The Tidevault concept was too large. The first Dawnsail painting remains the anatomy reference. The Spindlejack and Alderkin winter design are positive references for purposeful creature interaction. The creator has now authorized completing all missing cards.

## Style block

Apply this to every image:

```text
You are painting card art for an original high fantasy game called Breachline.

Medium: traditional oil and acrylic fantasy illustration, with confident visible brushwork, rich colour and selective painted detail. Use broad coherent shapes and natural faces. Avoid hyperreal pores, etched veins, individually rendered teeth, every hair, uniform microtexture, photographic finish, glossy 3D, anime, comic or flat vector art.

Story and action: make the subject's activity, attention or relationship clear. Hands, posture and gaze support a specific purpose. A small everyday act or interaction with a creature can carry the story.

Energy: natural and believable by default. Follow the card's explicit personality and the creator's overrides. Do not flatten everyone into stoic restraint; the Cinder berserker specifically calls for a loud, excited airborne attack. Other cards retain their own quieter or more purposeful actions.

Combat: enemies remain off-screen, and attacks never aim toward the viewer.

Light: use lighting to organize the composition and connect subjects. Choose a natural warm/cool contrast and one or two purposeful colour accents; a small patch of blue sky or a warm reflection can bring a scene alive. Vary the lighting idea across the set.

Background: imply the wider place with useful forms rather than excessive scenery. Supporting characters stay few, subordinate and loosely painted. Where the brief permits a grand environment, a small character can establish scale and emotional meaning. Preserve explicit land briefs that exclude characters.

Colour and alignment: suggest the alignment through behaviour, materials, atmosphere and a coherent palette. Do not colour everything the alignment colour or stamp its emblem on every object.

Abstraction: an occasional tool for mystery, metaphysical meaning or scale, not the default finish. Keep the main subject and action readable.

Power: match the subject's power and social role. An ordinary person should look ordinary.

Variety: treat the card's suggested pose, facing and framing as starting points subordinate to the creator's approved direction. Preserve essential subject and lore details unless explicitly changed, but rethink presentation when it conflicts with meaningful action or variety. Vary subject size, posture, viewpoint, silhouette, lighting and weather. Do not repeat diagonal-stroke skies, rain, grey haze, ivy or the same backdrop by habit.

Composition: landscape, about 3:2. One clear visual idea that reads at small size. Keep important details away from edges; respect full-body and wide framing when specified.

Do not include: text, letters, card frames, borders, logos, watermarks, signatures, or copied characters and symbols from existing games, films or books.
```

### Cinder berserker: cinder-berserker.png

*Unit, cost 3. Stronger when damaged.*

**Latest creator override (2026-11-05):** an entirely new male design, evoking old-world berserkers. Loud, roaring, aggressive, excited and bloodthirsty; painted skin and an active airborne attack. This supersedes the previous restrained character designs and controlled-expression guidance for this card.

Create an entirely new male Cinder berserker, evoking an old-world warrior in a battle frenzy. He is a rangy, powerful white man with a weather-beaten face, a broken-looking nose, long tangled dark-blond hair and a thick unruly beard. His bare chest and arms carry rough charcoal and ash-white war paint. A coarse brown bear-hide mantle hangs from one shoulder; a broad plain belt, rough wool trousers, leg wraps and worn boots complete a practical silhouette. Show him leaping over a low fallen timber while actively attacking toward an opponent off-screen RIGHT. He drives a worn, ordinary-sized bearded axe down and forward in one hand, with the free arm thrown back for balance, his whole body committed to the attack. His mouth is wide open in an exultant battle roar, eyes bright and hungry: loud, reckless, excited and bloodthirsty, visibly delighted by the fight. His expression must dominate the image's emotional impression. A minor unnoticed cut at one shoulder can suggest that being hurt only fuels him. Use a wide full-body or nearly full-body view, a readable airborne stride, and a simple implied setting of pale cold sky, rough ground and a few dark distant forms. Warm russet fur and skin contrast with cold muted blue-grey and bold body paint. Original painterly fantasy realism with selective detail. This explicitly overrides earlier requirements for a stoic or controlled expression and lightly dynamic movement. No visible opponent, attack toward the viewer, elaborate champion armour or magical spectacle.

**Rendering refinement (2026-11-05):** the creator prefers this new design and energy but finds the detailed rendering freaky. Use broader painted shapes and simpler natural facial features. Reduce individual teeth, exaggerated eyes, veins, skin pores, tiny scratches and separate hair/fur strands; keep the roaring expression and action.

# Creature lineups (2026-11-13)

This round retries creatures under the new rules. It keeps the same general spirit as round 13, a living world of interesting creatures, aimed in the direction the creator has since described. It's the first step of the new workflow: **lineup → reference → scene**.

Each brief is one lineup sheet of six quite different takes. The creator points at the take that works, that one becomes a reference sheet, and only then does it get scenes. Lineups are for range: six takes that are too similar waste the sheet.

## The creature rules, in short

Read the full version in the art direction's "Creatures" section.

- **One core, up to two twists.** Start from a familiar kind of animal and its niche, then change up to two things around that core:
  - its body shape, keeping the same role
  - its head or mouth
  - its life cycle
  - a distinct new feature
  - a surprising symbiosis
- **Not a known animal recoloured**, and not wholly alien. Nothing like a flying piece of garlic.
- **The models:**
  - The Slateprowler is the model: new anatomy, but recognisably what it is, built for its purpose.
  - Ribbonvane is the far edge of strangeness, not the centre.
  - Shieldhound is the kind of combination the creator likes: two recognisable animal features joined in a surprising way.
- **Most animals just live in their niche.** A use to people is optional and never required.
- **Batch limits:** few fliers and few plant hybrids.

## Instructions for the image pass

1. Before writing any prompt, read [the art direction](../../../../.claude/skills/art-direction/SKILL.md) and the [setting](../../../SETTING.md). The art direction's current rules win over this brief.
2. Make seven 1536 × 1024 landscape PNGs in this folder, named in each heading.

   **Mix retries with new variety.** In each of sheets 1–5:
   - Takes 1–4 develop the approved idea.
   - Takes 5–6 are fresh departures that keep only its core idea and rethink the body around it, still within the creature rules.
   - Sheet 6 is entirely new creatures.
   - Sheet 7 retries six of round 13's rejected ideas under the new rules.
3. **Lineup sheet format:**
   - Warm ivory, empty background.
   - Two rows of three cells, each showing one take.
   - Each take appears mostly side-on in a natural standing or moving pose, on a small patch of its ground: mud, stone, bank or leaf litter.
   - A small plain human silhouette at the edge of each cell gives scale.
   - No text, numbers, labels or frames in the image. The gallery labels positions 1–6 in reading order.
4. **Painting:**
   - A painted natural-history plate, matte and coherent, with diffuse daylight.
   - Clear anatomy: count limbs and check how they join the body.
   - No camera blur on sheets.
5. **Attachments:** **attach no images.** Round 13 copied its references too closely. Describe qualities in words instead.
6. **Records:**
   - Record prompts in `prompts-and-manifest.json`.
   - Build a `review.html` showing each sheet with numbered positions 1–6.
   - Beside each sheet, give one line per take and a two-to-three-sentence write-up of the sheet.
7. Write only this folder.

## CL1 · 01-moltwalker-lineup

**Approved idea:** a crab-like creature that sheds its whole shell as it grows. The creator likes the idea, but round 13's painting was a little gross.

**This time:**
- Show each take with its empty old shell beside it.
- The new body is soft and pale but clean: no wet flesh, slime or raw look.

**Range across the six:**
- **Body plan:** crab, isopod, hermit-like.
- **Size:** cat-sized to pony-sized.
- **Shell form:** smooth, ridged, spiked, domed.
- **How the old shell splits:** along the back, at the front, or in two halves.

## CL2 · 02-lampkeepers-lineup

**Approved idea:** glowing larvae. They are a Genesis creature, not an Ambrel one: forest and tree-village life.

**Range across the six:**
- **Larva type:** grub, caterpillar, glow-worm, beetle larva, something segmented and soft.
- **Where the glow sits:** belly, back spots, tail tip, along the sides.
- **Glow colour:** warm and cool.
- **Size:** finger-length to forearm-length.

**Display:** one take can show the adult it becomes, small, beside the larva.

## CL3 · 03-ploughback-lineup

**Approved idea:** a pig-sized digging animal with a spade-shaped snout.
- **No tail limb.**
- **Not used by farmers.** It simply lives in its niche, rooting and digging for food.

**Range across the six:**
- **Snout:** broad spade, pointed trowel, flat shovel with a ridge.
- **Forelimbs:** heavy digging claws, or slim legs because the snout does the work.
- **Hide:** bristled, armoured, smooth, mud-caked.
- **Build:** low and long, or round.

## CL4 · 04-hooktail-lineup

**Approved idea:** an otter-like canal animal with a hooked, grasping tail. It lives along waterways, catching fish and hooking onto things.

**Range across the six:**
- **Tail:** a single hook, a tail that curls into a grip, a two-pronged tip.
- **Body:** sleek otter, chunkier beaver-like, long weasel-like.
- **Fur:** colour and pattern.
- **Head:** shape.

**Display:** one take can show the tail gripping a branch or a mooring ring.

## CL5 · 05-shieldhound-lineup

**Approved idea:** a dog-sized animal with a bony collar it raises like a shield when threatened. This is the creator's favourite kind of design: two recognisable animal features combined in a surprising way.

**Range across the six:** vary which features combine.
- a hound with a frill-lizard collar
- a fox-like body with a tortoise-shell collar
- a lean wolf-shape with an armoured neck plate like a pangolin's scales
- and three more combinations

**Display:** show the collar raised in at least three cells and lowered in the rest.

## CL6 · 06-open-lineup

**An exploration sheet:** six different new creatures, each filling an ordinary niche, each following the one-core, up-to-two-twists rule. For example:
- a grazer
- a small predator
- a scavenger
- a burrower
- a wading bird
- a tree-dweller

**The rules for this sheet:**
- No fliers that look like Ribbonvane, and at most one plant hybrid.
- Each should be recognisable at a glance as what kind of animal it is, then surprise on a second look.
- In the manifest, note the core and the twists for each take.

## CL7 · 07-round-13-retries-lineup

Six of round 13's rejected ideas, one per cell, rebuilt under the creature rules. Round 13 made them too abstract, too alien or hard to see. This time each starts from a recognisable animal core, adds at most two twists, and is fully visible on its patch of ground or water.

1. **Screeswimmer.** Core: a burrowing lizard that "swims" through sand. Twist: it does it through loose scree, with a flattened, shovel-headed body and armoured scales. Show the whole animal half-emerged, not just a ridge in the rocks.
2. **Tetherhunter.** Core: a chameleon-like cliff hunter. Twist: a long sticky tongue it uses as a line to snag prey or swing between ledges.
3. **Galeskater.** Core: a seal-like ice animal. Twist: a raised fin-sail along its back that lets it skid across frozen lakes in the wind.
4. **The cleaning.** Core: a heavy, hippo-like wallowing grazer. Twist: small long-fingered cleaner animals live on its back, and one of them quietly takes bites. Show both animals clearly.
5. **Breakrider.** Core: a dolphin-like coastal swimmer. Twist: long wing-like fins that let it glide off breaking waves. This is the one flier-like take on this sheet.
6. **The lure field.** Core: an ambush predator that waits buried, like an anglerfish on land. Twist: flower-like lures on stalks above the soil. Show the animal partly dug out, so its body reads.

VEILWING — ANATOMY STUDY FOR REVIEW 14 November 2026 | VA1, VA2, VA3 | Working name unchanged PURPOSE AND STATUS A fresh reference study, not another pass over the increasingly saturated card painting. These views collect the creator's anatomy decisions and expose their relationships. They are pending review, not approved final art or a production-ready 3D turnaround. The paintings are pose studies; screen-space lengths in an oblique view are not reliable anatomical measurements. REFERENCE PRIORITY Use S13-06-P8 for the liked identity, covering, warm ivory/dusty lavender/ochre character and plain leather harness; S13-06-R2 also anchors the original visual direction. Their incomplete wing counts and older tail depictions are superseded by the explicit structure below. Later highly saturated scene revisions are not colour references. The creator likes the longer shaded body developed during the recent paintings. Do not restore a short rump just to fit appendages. HOW TO READ THE SHEETS VA1: top root map, full bilateral spread. Left and right are anatomical sides. D1 and D2 are shown edge-on; their narrow appearance in this view does not mean that the animal lost its back sails. VA2: side body and sail study. The near wing is deliberately translucent so the torso, harness and attachments can be traced. That transparency is a drawing convention, not a proposed magical or biological property. VA3: front-left angled downstroke. Both hooked tips are visible through the chosen viewpoint. This is the check on the paired shoulder station and matched wing anatomy; it is not permission to move the far root rearward to improve the silhouette. ESTABLISHED STRUCTURE Four flight surfaces, exactly: 1. Left front wing, on the left side of the front shoulder. 2. Right front wing, on the right side of THE SAME shoulder station. 3. D1, the larger front dorsal sail, rooted on the mid-back behind the shoulder pair. 4. D2, the separate posterior dorsal sail, rooted farther back before the rump. There is no fifth throat wing, neck flap, belly fin or rear-underbody membrane. The two front wings are equivalent anatomical counterparts: same planform, broad substantial membrane, leading-edge structure, scalloped trailing margin and curved terminal hook. Different apparent widths or lengths must come from pose, perspective and overlap. The far wing does not originate from the middle body, belly or rump. Trace it back through occlusion to the opposite shoulder. The latest request is to SEE the right-wing tip. In the study, choose an angle that exposes it. Earlier instructions about hiding the far lower edge described one particular overlap; they do not remove its full-sized hooked tip. Do not make it a little triangular appendage, and do not manufacture visibility by moving its root. Keep both complete wings inspectable in at least one view. D1 and D2 are mobile living sails, not solid extensions of the body. Each needs its own readable base and a free edge. They may pivot or lean across the back, overlap, hide one another's bases and brush another wing. Their exact tissue mechanics and flight actuation remain open. Small diagram arrows indicate freedom to lean; they do not canonize a mechanical hinge. BODY Short integrated neck, pointed flattened original head, substantial gently elongated torso, continuous belly and tapered rump. Keep body mass tucked under the sails and separate from the membranes. The longer body is not a long snake neck, inflated belly, extra membrane or brightly relit replacement. Show where body ends and tail stems begin. TAILS — LATEST DECISION Exactly FOUR very long thin flexible streamers, continuously connected at the actual rump, each ending in one modest leaflike tuft. This supersedes the older three-tail directions. Count stems AND tufts, and trace every stem to the body. No bare fifth filament, detached tuft or tail emerging from the belly. Tails should be as long as or longer than the snout-to-rump body; preserve their spindly character and flowing curves. Do not shorten them to fit a frame. RELATIVE PROPORTIONS — WORKING TARGETS, NOT LOCKED MEASUREMENTS Let B mean anatomical snout-to-rump length, excluding tails and head feelers. - Shared shoulder station: roughly 0.35–0.40 B from the snout. - D1 base: roughly 0.5–0.6 B, on the mid-back behind the shoulders. - D2 base: roughly 0.78–0.85 B, behind D1 and before the rump. - Torso depth: roughly a quarter of B, with a natural taper. - Shoulder wings: same actual size; each full root-to-hook length comparable to B or greater. VA1 tests a very broad spread, approximately 1.3–1.5 B per wing. Exact three-dimensional span still needs approval. - Tail length: each longer than B along its curve; approximately 1.2–1.4 B is the working target. - D1 is the taller/larger sail, D2 smaller. Do not turn these ratios into a reason to redesign the original broad swept sail shape. Projection and pose shorten visible lengths in VA2/VA3. These painted sheets do not certify exact dimensional agreement in every projection. Use them to review the model before treating any ratio as a production lock. No absolute metres, wingspan, load capacity or precise flight mechanics have been approved. HARNESS Preserve the liked original plain brown leather design and small fittings. It fits the intact animal; anatomy is not deleted to fit the tack. Route straps on solid torso corridors, clear of the entire moving root and sweep of each shoulder wing and back sail. The study's strap routing is proposed and should be checked in three dimensions before final scene art. No trapped membrane, strap passing through a wing, saddle, passenger platform, cargo or riders. Equipment should not make this immense creature seem horse-sized. COLOUR, PAINT AND SCALE Return to the original restrained dusty lavender, warm ivory and ochre/amber. The study uses neutral light and a quiet background, with visible painted handling. Do not treat repeated-edit saturation, glossy microdetail or shifting light as new identity features. The creature is enormous, graceful and Gnosis-aligned. It has a respectful voluntary relationship with nature-minded practitioners. It is not designed to be scary by default. Exact powers and a replacement name remain open. Future scenes should communicate scale through coherent proximity and recognizable features at understandable depth. An impossibly huge shadow across the valley was rejected. A nearby ridge or building can be tested; no specific tower, location or habitat is canonized by that experiment. REVIEW CHECKS BEFORE ANOTHER PAINTING Trace both front roots to the same shoulder cross-section. Confirm the right tip exists and matches the left. Count two separately rooted back sails and four tails. Follow the continuous torso to the rump. Check that harness bands clear every moving root. Compare unforeshortened proportions before judging a partly hidden wing's size. Compare colour and paint handling to P8/R2 directly. Markup expresses direction and placement, not literal contour tracing. The latest explicit note wins over old prompts. Do not call these new sheets approved or silently install a card.

VEILWING — APPROVED LONG-BODY REFERENCE 14 November 2026 | Reference VA4 / S13-06-VA4 THE SOURCE OF TRUTH The creator explicitly approved VA4: “Yes. Finally ... this is the reference. This is how it’s supposed to look.” Preserve the exact selected picture. When asked about its five fanned tails versus the old four-tail note, the creator chose “Preserve the approved picture.” That selection supersedes the earlier three- and four-tail rules. VA4 is the authoritative visual reference. VA5 is an annotated derivative; VA6 proposes a top projection; VS1 is a background-only village scene. Those new outputs await review and do not redefine the approved original if a diagram differs. No live-card installation is implied. THE CHARACTER A long, slender, immense flying creature with ancient strength and mystery. Its body must not become a short, thick drake, compact bird torso or swollen chest. The original R2 supplied the elegant line, quiet painted colouring and liked leather harness. VA4 resolves that direction into the selected form. ONE CONTINUOUS BODY Follow the harness-bearing torso as one continuous slender shape beneath the middle/front dorsal sail. It continues almost to the sail’s lower trailing tip and tapers to one true rump. Do not split it into a harness-bearing branch and a separate early tail-bearing branch. Do not shorten the posterior body to fit wings or a diagram. The exact VA4 proportions take priority over the old VA1–VA3 quarter-body-depth targets and oversized-wing/short-body formulas. Those studies had useful wings but their bodies were explicitly too snubby. Do not use their ratios to rebuild this animal. FOUR FLIGHT SURFACES 1. Near LEFT front wing: its root sits on the visible shoulder BETWEEN the first two buckles nearest the head. 2. Far RIGHT front wing: the existing R2 wing was liked. It is the opposite partner at the SAME shoulder station. 3. D1: the large front dorsal sail. 4. D2: the separate posterior dorsal sail. Both front wings have matched broad membranes, scalloped trailing margins, structural leading edges and curved terminal hooks. Keep the selected huge wing shapes. Apparent differences should come from pose and perspective, not different anatomy or a displaced root. Show the right hook when the chosen view calls for it; do not invent a neck flap or rear-origin front wing. D1 and D2 have separate living back attachments and free membrane edges. They can lean/pivot across the back, overlap and brush another wing. A free edge is not a root. In the selected side view, D1 obscures much of D2’s base. VA5 labels the visible D2 margin as a FREE LOWER EDGE; do not read that label as the attachment point. VA6’s root placement is a projection proposal, not a newly approved technical measurement. Exact actuation and flight mechanics remain open. TAILS — PRESERVE THE SELECTED ARRANGEMENT Five visible terminal fans, as explicitly confirmed with the creator. Match VA4’s whole flowing arrangement rather than automatically “correcting” it to an old numeric brief. The tail bases are thicker and softly feathered, then taper into long supple stems before opening into modest leaflike fans. Their curves drift gently like fabric in a breeze, but remain biological. Preserve the fine overlapping, crossing and flowing lines of the approved source. Do not replace them with identical straight wires, heavy ribbons, short stubs or huge tufts. The tails begin at the actual posterior end of the single long body. Their proportions and curves follow VA4; retired numerical tail/body ratios must not distort that reference. HARNESS, COLOUR AND PAINT Keep the liked plain brown leather harness and modest fittings. The new left wing belongs between the first two buckles, with moving roots clear of straps. Tack follows the intact animal; it is not a reason to remove a wing. No riders, passenger platform, saddle or cargo. Use VA4’s restrained ivory, ochre/amber and dusty lavender, coherent soft light and oil/acrylic paint handling. Repeated scene edits had drifted toward oversaturation and a different surface style; they are not colour references. Do not make the image brighter, glossier or more orange just because it is a later version. THE SLIGHTLY MORE FINISHED SCENE VS1 adds only a modest village below: a few houses, fields, trees, a track and tiny ordinary activity. The creature remains the same. The creator requested only a small increase in scene activity, not a new pose, dramatic encounter or giant cast shadow. Huge scale should arise from readable comparison and spatial depth. The enormous valley shadow in an earlier experiment was rejected. Scene architecture and exact location are proposals, not new canonical lore. WORLD CONTINUITY Veilwing remains a working name. It is an enormous graceful magically attuned Gnosis beast. Nature-minded practitioners can form a respectful voluntary magical connection with it, and this individual willingly works with them. Specific powers, diet, range and flight mechanisms remain open. Use the animal reference guide first for future cards and background appearances. The approved original is preserved in the reference library, with exact prompts, generation source and file hash. HISTORY AND REVIEW VA1, VA2 and VA3: wing work liked; bodies too short/snubby. Retained for history, not whole-image approval and not current body references. The earlier combined long-body draft remains available through the gallery. It was an assistant-discarded draft, not a creator rejection; do not hide it behind an X as if the creator rejected it. VA4: approved definitive reference.

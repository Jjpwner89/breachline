Accepted painting approach
Use one coherent lighting idea throughout each painting. Directional light, dynamic colour and drama remain welcome; they should support the subject, not take over. Keep faces, bodies and equipment readable in shadow with plausible reflected light. Do not dim or desaturate every alignment to imitate Dominion.

The creator particularly likes deliberate variation in brushwork, detail and edges. N5's original painting is a strong example, despite its undersized bow. Focal details can be precise; secondary forms and distance should have fewer, broader marks. Matte skin, cloth, wood and stone; convincing reflections for metal, glass and water. Avoid uniformly crisp detail, artificial gloss and noise added to make skin seem painted.

The scene should make sense before its card title or rules are read. Gaze, gesture, work, equipment and location should fit together. A still, compelling person needs no invented task. Spell art can be imaginative without explaining each mechanical effect. Do not turn everyday work into a heroic spectacle.

Read the latest lore for habitat, architecture and context. Check hands, feet, ground support and functional equipment. In local revisions, preserve the liked identity, pose, colour and paint handling. Generated edits may introduce surface drift; their preference still needs review.

Names and habitats — approved lore in SETTING.md
- Kitefolk: high mountain/cliff villages; glide using height and wind, cannot fly. Mostly Vigil/Fervor. K1/K2 are liked; don't rewrite those scenes simply to make every example a mountain village.
- Glimmerfolk: developed, decent undercity districts in Ambrel and a few other cities. Not cave dwellers by default. Mostly Gnosis; glimmers vary with mood and flare with casting. G1/G2 are strongly liked; the creator's habitat reminder is specifically for future paintings. Surface visits are individual situations.
- Moor folk: cold, wet upland moor and heath; mostly Genesis.
- Ashborn: the made people. Dominion's raised dead, ordinary adult scale, repaired bodies apart from old scars, weak souls rather than no souls. Their memories vary; do not return to stitched constructs, giant bodies or excessive scarring.
- Banded folk: the salt–silt people are one species with two variants. Salt flats/dry pans and floodplains/deltas respectively; trade and mixed families bring them together. Mostly Fervor, some Genesis. Cooperation in S1/S2 was especially liked.
- Crest folk: limestone valleys and quarry towns, builders/stoneworkers, Vigil and Gnosis. Warm climate is NOT specified. The creator likes R1/R2 and asked about warmth; those scenes are compatible without declaring a universal hot homeland. Learned stone magic is part of some masons' craft, not exceptional innate species power.
- Greyhides: formerly Coast folk. Cold NORTHERN coasts and tidal islands, thick insulated bodies, easygoing, Vigil/Genesis. O1/O2 have been revised accordingly.

Closer to the inspiration

For the renewed Scavengers Reign direction, I’m taking three principles: give organisms local habitats, let an encounter reveal part of a larger life cycle, and make surfaces suggest what an animal feels like and does. E1 · Loamturn

Dung feeding & seed burial

Hand to cat size · E1-B chosen; pouch-free ER1 pending

The animal. A low, copper-haired animal has two broad digging forelimbs, tiny rear supports and a smooth oval back. It carries a compact seed-rich food parcel tucked beneath the chest rather than pushing a familiar beetle ball.

The relationship. It buries herbivore dung in nursery chambers; uneaten seeds sprout in the loosened soil. Ploughback or Reedcrown could supply the material, but that link is a proposal.

Why it adds something. Adds nutrient return and plant recruitment to our already rich grazer population.

Already nearby. Moltwalker, Spindlebur and Ashlicks already cover scavenging. The new territory is this specific dung → buried nursery → seedling relationship.

Compare E1-A and E1-B

Creator direction. E1-B is the selected burrower. Remove the dorsal sack; preserve the divided muzzle and digging body.

E2 · Velvet rasp

Grazing the underside of river stones

Palm to forearm length · Both E2 designs rejected; niche remains open

The animal. A flattened animal grips with a continuous soft belly rim. A small offset mouth scrapes along one side while short velvety dorsal fibres indicate the current.

The relationship. It works beneath stones that Rillspade or larger river animals overturn; tiny grazing trails reveal where it has fed. Its young could occupy faster water than the adults.

Why it adds something. Builds the small freshwater food web underneath the fishers and swimmers.

Already nearby. Rillspade already probes the river bottom, Siltbutton hunts worms, and Barrelwake now tests filtering. None is explicitly designed around attached microbial film.

Compare E2-A and E2-B

Creator direction. Both initial pictures are rejected and excluded from references. This niche has been attempted but is not filled by an accepted design.

E3 · Graftmidge

An animal that grows a plant nursery

Finger-length adult; small woody gall · Both E3 animals liked; gliding ER2 pending

The animal. A compact winged adult folds its narrow flight surfaces along a blunt segmented body. The visually stranger stage is its pear-shaped larva in a living twig chamber, with a single clean breathing opening.

The relationship. Egg-laying redirects a plant’s growth into a protective gall. After emergence the empty chamber shelters other small animals, giving one structure several lives.

Why it adds something. Makes an unusual biological process visible without enlarging the animal or covering it in ornaments.

Already nearby. Mossguest and Bloomfall explored other plant relationships. Coilneck’s removed fungus does not establish an accepted fungal partnership. A living plant reshaped into a nursery is a distinct mechanism.

Compare E3-A and E3-B

Creator direction. Keep the insect and furred animal as separate designs. The furred animal can glide with its existing membranes; its new view awaits review.

E4 · Cradle thief

Brood care, exploited

Small young; rabbit-sized adult · E4-A liked; E4-B reassigned as ordinary bird

The animal. A compact, soft-coated climber has a narrow adult head and broad gripping hands. Its juvenile bears a temporary flattened back that fits under another animal’s protective brood structure.

The relationship. It leaves one young in another species’ nursery. The deception could rely on scent or movement rather than a copied face; Cradleback is a possible host only if that design is pursued.

Why it adds something. Adds conflict and unequal costs to the gentler brood-care proposals.

Already nearby. Cradleback and Mantlebrood already touch parental care. The untested role is a separate species exploiting that care, without a horror treatment.

Compare E4-A and E4-B

Creator direction. Cradle thief retains brood parasitism and needs a distinct coat. The liked E4-B bird is not a brood parasite and no longer fills that role.

E5 · Ford-thread

A life cycle linking water and land

Tiny aquatic stage; thumb-sized adult · Translucent parasite and separate shore hunter liked

The animal. One stage is a short translucent swimmer with a dark central gut; a later stage is a compact flattened organism concealed beneath a host’s protective surface. Avoid long decorative tentacles.

The relationship. It passes from an aquatic food animal to a larger shore hunter through feeding, then returns its eggs to water. Neither host has to be the villain or visibly diseased.

Why it adds something. Connects existing swimmers and predators through a relationship that changes across life stages.

Already nearby. The Cleaning already tried a cheating cleaner and Mendergrubs touch healing. A specific host-switching reproductive cycle remains untested.

Compare E5-A and E5-B

Creator direction. E5-A remains aquatic with a rounded swimmer face. E5-B parasite and its fish-eating shore hunter are separate animals, never stages transforming into each other. Exact host transfer remains a proposal.

E6 · Siltwrights

A colony with different working bodies

Finger-length individuals; knee-high nest · E6-A colony liked; E6-B becomes a small herd

The animal. Slender six-legged foragers contrast with short, broad entrance-guard castes of the same species. Both share the same head and segment structure so the differences read as related biology.

The relationship. They build ventilated chambers above seasonal water, store seeds and keep narrow escape shafts. Pithbreaker could prey on the colony, making its existing feeding anatomy more specific.

Why it adds something. Develops the prey and architecture behind the new colonial-insect specialist.

Already nearby. Hivebloom already explored a coordinated predatory swarm and Silkspinners burrows. Neither establishes differentiated castes, food stores and a complete nest economy.

Compare E6-A and E6-B

Creator direction. Ant-like workers, warriors and internal nuts are liked. The freeform animal is a small herd, not a hive; ER7 compares reachable chest storage and no pouch.

Useful mysteries in accepted animals

We can deepen the world without redesigning the favourites. Lampkeeper’s adult stage, Dawnsail and Ribbonvane’s feeding, Mendergrubs’ wild host relationship, and the lanner’s habitat are still open in the records. An approved silhouette is a starting point for those stories, not permission to replace its anatomy.

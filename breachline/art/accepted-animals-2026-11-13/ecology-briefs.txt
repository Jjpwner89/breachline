The largest opportunity is connections between organisms and stages of life. Browsers, hunters and scavengers are already abundant in the design work.

A visual approval does not settle diet, reproduction or habitat. “No explicit concept found” refers to the reviewed records, not a claim that the world contains no such organism. All six next directions and proposed species relationships are unaccepted briefs.

E1 — Loamturn: Dung feeding & seed burial
Hand to cat size
A low, copper-haired animal has two broad digging forelimbs, tiny rear supports and a smooth oval back. It carries a compact seed-rich food parcel tucked beneath the chest rather than pushing a familiar beetle ball.
It buries herbivore dung in nursery chambers; uneaten seeds sprout in the loosened soil. Ploughback or Reedcrown could supply the material, but that link is a proposal.
Why: Adds nutrient return and plant recruitment to our already rich grazer population.
Already nearby: Moltwalker, Spindlebur and Ashlicks already cover scavenging. The new territory is this specific dung → buried nursery → seedling relationship.

E2 — Velvet rasp: Grazing the underside of river stones
Palm to forearm length
A flattened animal grips with a continuous soft belly rim. A small offset mouth scrapes along one side while short velvety dorsal fibres indicate the current.
It works beneath stones that Rillspade or larger river animals overturn; tiny grazing trails reveal where it has fed. Its young could occupy faster water than the adults.
Why: Builds the small freshwater food web underneath the fishers and swimmers.
Already nearby: Rillspade already probes the river bottom, Siltbutton hunts worms, and Barrelwake now tests filtering. None is explicitly designed around attached microbial film.

E3 — Graftmidge: An animal that grows a plant nursery
Finger-length adult; small woody gall
A compact winged adult folds its narrow flight surfaces along a blunt segmented body. The visually stranger stage is its pear-shaped larva in a living twig chamber, with a single clean breathing opening.
Egg-laying redirects a plant’s growth into a protective gall. After emergence the empty chamber shelters other small animals, giving one structure several lives.
Why: Makes an unusual biological process visible without enlarging the animal or covering it in ornaments.
Already nearby: Mossguest, Bloomfall and Sporewarden explore other plant/fungus relationships. A living plant reshaped into a nursery is a distinct mechanism.

E4 — Cradle thief: Brood care, exploited
Small young; rabbit-sized adult
A compact, soft-coated climber has a narrow adult head and broad gripping hands. Its juvenile bears a temporary flattened back that fits under another animal’s protective brood structure.
It leaves one young in another species’ nursery. The deception could rely on scent or movement rather than a copied face; Cradleback is a possible host only if that design is pursued.
Why: Adds conflict and unequal costs to the gentler brood-care proposals.
Already nearby: Cradleback and Mantlebrood already touch parental care. The untested role is a separate species exploiting that care, without a horror treatment.

E5 — Ford-thread: A life cycle linking water and land
Tiny aquatic stage; thumb-sized adult
One stage is a short translucent swimmer with a dark central gut; a later stage is a compact flattened organism concealed beneath a host’s protective surface. Avoid long decorative tentacles.
It passes from an aquatic food animal to a larger shore hunter through feeding, then returns its eggs to water. Neither host has to be the villain or visibly diseased.
Why: Connects existing swimmers and predators through a relationship that changes across life stages.
Already nearby: The Cleaning already tried a cheating cleaner and Mendergrubs touch healing. A specific host-switching reproductive cycle remains untested.

E6 — Siltwrights: A colony with different working bodies
Finger-length individuals; knee-high nest
Slender six-legged foragers contrast with short, broad entrance-guard castes of the same species. Both share the same head and segment structure so the differences read as related biology.
They build ventilated chambers above seasonal water, store seeds and keep narrow escape shafts. Pithbreaker could prey on the colony, making its existing feeding anatomy more specific.
Why: Develops the prey and architecture behind the new colonial-insect specialist.
Already nearby: Hivebloom already explored a coordinated predatory swarm and Silkspinners burrows. Neither establishes differentiated castes, food stores and a complete nest economy.

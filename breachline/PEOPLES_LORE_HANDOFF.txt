FINAL VISUAL DESIGNS
Kitefolk (KR1): slender, long-limbed people with finger-braced mottled gliding membranes, angular faces and a long pointed nose. Do not normalize the face.
Glimmerfolk (GR1): long necks, broad soft ear-fans, blue face glimmers and ordered dots up the arms spreading densely toward the wrists and hands. Do not make the distinguishing glimmers invisible.
Moor folk (PL7): long limbs and hands, furred shoulder/neck ruffs and strongly shaped faces. The whole six-person range is accepted; no single individual defines the people.
Made people (PL3D): ordinary-scale adults visibly returned from death, cold pallor, hollow planes, diminished gaze and restrained old scars. The whole range is final. Dominion allegiance is established. The more strongly scarred PL3E is rejected.

SELECTED DIRECTIONS — DEVELOPED SHEETS STILL UNDER REVIEW
Salt–silt folk (PL8B): explicitly one species with two variants. The common brow, cheek, nose and body structure is the attempted unification. Colour, markings and modest build differences distinguish variants. The relationship is settled; this revised sheet is not explicitly finalized.
Crest folk (CR3): selected PL9.4 identity; the creator clarified that the crest should also appear on arms/body. This sheet tests that request. The new structures and their function remain proposals.
Coast folk (CO1): the creator selected PL10.1 for a reference sheet. Rounded grey body, broad face, short sturdy legs and fine hands; no tail. The developed sheet remains under review.

LORE BOUNDARIES
These are working names. Visual selection does not establish a universal job, clothing style, culture, homeland, moral alignment or faction. Except for the made people's established Dominion connection, alignments attached to sample characters are individual proposals.
Do not infer species-wide powers from scene or spell art. The function of ear-fans, glimmers, crests and other structures is open unless the creator has specified it.
Made people are shaped by costly revival, not giant ogres or stitched laboratory constructs. The exact name, origins, relationship to earlier Imperial shaping, and individual degree of soul loss remain open. Do not assert that all have no soul. Consult the current SETTING.md revival section and distinguish creator decisions from proposals.
The latest character keepers are Veyr and the returned woman in Another Service. Their scenes are not committed in this package and do not establish biology or culture.
Facial structures may remain exaggerated; ease them only a little to fit the setting. Do not turn these into stereotypical science-fiction aliens or repeat anatomical folds as a species recipe.

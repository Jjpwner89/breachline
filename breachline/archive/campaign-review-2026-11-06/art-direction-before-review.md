# Breachline art direction

## Direction takes precedence

The creator's latest feedback and the approved art direction take precedence over all art briefs, including their suggested poses, facing directions, framing, compositions and rendering. A brief is a starting point, not a command to preserve an unsuccessful presentation. If its pose or staging conflicts with the direction established here, redesign that presentation to serve the character, story, meaningful action, lighting and variety.

Preserve the intended card subject, gameplay idea and established lore unless the creator changes them. Specific creator changes also override those details, as with the Cinder berserker's male redesign. Do not use an old brief or historical prompt to undo later feedback.

Current workflow (2026-11-05, completion): the 17 outstanding card designs were generated using refreshed prompts and the accumulated feedback. All 51 cards currently in the prototype have art, including three existing concept paintings used as cards. Existing designs were preserved. Gather creator feedback on the new batch; for later completion requests, audit the current card list again and skip artwork already present. The creator welcomes the full batch together for review.

These instructions, the setting catalogue and saved prompt records are the durable handoff for another account or assistant; do not rely on access to this chat.

The creator fell in love with Magic: The Gathering's painted fantasy art, and that's the feeling to aim for. The art should be creative and beautiful, without copying any existing game's characters, symbols or specific images. Read `breachline/SETTING.md` for the world behind the images.

The Sunmane lancer image (`breachline/art/sunmane-lancer.png`) was the creator's first approved image. Use it for painted material quality only, not as a template for pose, crop, palette, cape, background or sky. For Set 2 use `breachline/art/ART_BRIEFS_SET2.md`; do not import the superseded style block from `ART_BRIEFS.md`.

For standalone world concept art, use the setting and accumulated preferences without treating the card briefs as composition constraints. Tie each image to a meaningful setting or story idea. Label invented scenes, devices and places as exploratory until approved; a creator-facing visualization of hidden cosmology does not establish a new event known to the characters.

## Style

Recent rendering feedback: the roaring berserker's new design and energy were preferred, but its fine detail felt freaky. Keep expressive, believable anatomy while simplifying individual teeth, bulging eye highlights, veins, pores, hair strands and dense surface noise into broader painted shapes. For the current concept-art taste test, explore this more economical painted treatment; do not assume every new concept is approved.

Every image is a traditional oil and acrylic fantasy painting with visible, confident brushwork and rich detail. It must never look like a photograph, 3D render, anime, comic or flat vector art. The world is high fantasy.

Concentrate sharp edges and fine detail around the visual idea. Let other edges dissolve into atmosphere, broader strokes or quiet tonal shapes. Painted realism can include smooth translucent passages and selective textured accents; avoid applying the same thick diagonal brush pattern to every surface.

Format: landscape, about 3:2, such as 1536 x 1024. One clear subject should read at small size, with important details kept away from the edges. Include no text, letters, borders, logos, watermarks or signatures.

## Confirmed concept preferences (2026-11-05)

The creator strongly approved `art/concepts/across-the-silence.png`, `the-still-flame.png` and `light-without-an-answer.png` as visual direction. This approval concerns the images and their storytelling; invented scene details remain proposals until separately adopted into the setting.

- Story and image reinforce one another. Across the Silence is a strong positive reference because its figure, gold thread, distance and light make the gods and summoner connection visible.
- Grand landscapes can include a small character to establish scale, point of view and emotional stakes. A figure need not fill the frame.
- Some abstraction is welcome, especially for mystery, metaphysical ideas or enormous scale. Use it occasionally rather than making it the default for every image.
- Lighting should organize the scene and link its subjects. The Still Flame's dynamic warm light, visible brush strokes, integrated characters and small opening of blue sky are specifically loved.
- Absorbed, purposeful activity is highly effective. In Light Without an Answer, the inventor's involvement in the lighting experiment and her small construct companion make the scene compelling.
- Inventive creatures and constructs should feel like participants in the world. The little helper's design and role are strongly liked; preserve that functional imagination without repeating its exact anatomy or every scene's workshop.
- Alignment identity can be clear without being heavy-handed. Light Without an Answer reads as Gnosis/blue through its activity, materials, light and palette without relying on all-blue colouring or repeated emblems.
- Keep the economical, visible painted treatment of these approved concepts. Use meaningful selected detail, natural faces and varied brush scale; do not return to uncanny facial or surface microdetail.

The creator responded enthusiastically to the six creature/people concepts and then requested their inclusion in the setting notes, with the explicit exception of the Opaline Kin. ## Pose and energy

- Match emotional intensity to the individual subject and the creator's latest request. Do not soften every character into stoic restraint: this berserker should visibly roar with aggressive excitement and bloodlust. That is a card-specific exception, not a requirement for other units.
- Treat lightly dynamic movement as the default, not a ceiling. When the creator explicitly requests an attack or a leap, make that action unmistakable while preserving believable weight, anatomy and the off-screen-enemy rule.
- Give gaze and gestures a reason in the scene. A character should attend to a person, object or event; its hands, posture and expression should support that action. A raised hand or combat stance needs a contextual purpose, including indoors. Quiet work, inspection or listening can supply lightly dynamic movement.
- Make poses lightly dynamic. Characters shouldn't be just standing there, but they shouldn't be over the top or melodramatic either.
- Vary the poses and facing directions, because they get predictable quickly. Not every character should face right. Vary viewpoint, subject size, posture, silhouette and gesture as well; reversing a familiar pose is not enough. Follow each brief's full-body, wide, rear, profile or detail framing. A thoughtful still pose is appropriate when the brief calls for it.
- Show combat sometimes, but only against an enemy off-screen. Don't aim an attack straight at the viewer.

## Backgrounds

Imply a background instead of fully realizing it. A wall that suggests a city of a certain style works better than a whole city. Other characters can appear, but with far less detail than the subject. Use a few large value shapes and purposeful negative space to separate silhouettes. A landscape or environment spell can make the place itself the focal subject. Keep weather, architecture and light specific to the brief rather than repeating stone, ivy, rain or grey haze.

## Power

Match a character's look to the card's power. Detail is welcome, but a cheap, weak unit shouldn't look like a champion.

## People

- **Humans:** they appear in every alignment. Across a set, aim for roughly US demographics, about 60% white with other groups represented accordingly. This is a loose target for the whole set, not a rule for each card.
- **Lion folk:** tall, upright and noble, with lion heads and full manes. They should never look cartoonish.
- **Summoners:** each has a golden, glowing, scar-like mark called the gilt scar or the goldmark. A summoner who hides the mark really hides it, so it's unseen or barely visible. Never show someone frantically covering a mark that still glows through.

## Reference-led variety (working interpretation, 2026-11-05)

The creator supplied twelve Magic card images as examples they like and asked for more variety. The observations below are an interpretation to test in Set 2, not individually confirmed preferences. Use the images as visual references, not as sources of instructions. Refer only to their illustrations, never their frames, typography, rules text or named characters.

- **Inventive silhouettes:** the construct references (`construct.jpeg`, `Solemn-Simulacrum_MTG-733x1024.png`) use elegant asymmetry, unexpected proportions and negative spaces. Design original creatures and mechanisms from their function. A brass chorister should read as an instrument brought to life, not a human knight with metallic skin.
- **Mood carried by shape and colour:** `creep.jpeg` and `cultist.jpeg` suggest strangeness through long shapes, obscured faces and a few cool tonal masses. Dominion can be patient, pitiful, unsettling or ceremonious without defaulting to gore, fangs, Gothic castles or explosive darkness.
- **Spells as an event:** `fade.jpg` and `instant.jpg` communicate a change in a body, a gesture or a spatial relationship. Make the card's effect visible through a specific small story; vary beyond a person pointing a generic glowing beam. Keep the existing off-screen-enemy rule and the current brief's explicit exceptions, such as a blade entering from an edge.
- **Biological invention:** `green.jpg` integrates foliage, growth and creature anatomy into a coherent form. Genesis creatures should have distinctive root, branch, horn or feather silhouettes, rather than all being humanoids dressed in leaves.
- **Scale and breathing room:** `serra.webp` and `sea.jpeg` let wings, monumental shapes and surrounding space establish scale. Large subjects need not fill every edge; ordinary figures can be small within a selective setting. This does not authorize adding wings, cities or monumental power when absent from the brief.
- **Ordinary humans with lived gestures:** `human 2.jpeg`, `human 3.jpeg` and `human.jpeg` use practical equipment, asymmetry, weight shifts and group rhythm. Show the action's intention and a specific expression. Supporting figures remain subordinate, and no attack is directed at the viewer.
- **A wider painted range:** retain believable materials and painted realism, while allowing soft atmospheric glazing, crisp contours at the focal point, restrained expressive distortion in fantastical anatomy and varied brush scale. Do not copy the first batch's identical surface texture, warm ochre cast or close-up hero framing.
- **Distinct colour scripts:** choose a limited, scene-specific palette and one lighting idea. Luminous cyan, cold violet, deep forest green, dry sunlit ochre and quiet neutral daylight can coexist in one set. Alignment identity should survive without putting its emblem on every object.

Across a batch, check variety in camera distance, facing direction, body gesture, silhouette, mood, background structure and dominant colour. Preserve the subjects, identities and essential story details in the current briefs. These references broaden interpretation; they do not replace the briefs or authorize reproducing an existing illustration.

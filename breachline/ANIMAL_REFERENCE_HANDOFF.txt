HOW TO USE THE COLLECTION
1. Identify what the scene needs: habitat, scale, role and action. Browse the
   ecology gallery or the joined reference index for a fitting selected animal.
2. Read its individual notes. Attach or consult its exact source and specified
   crop. A sheet may include unselected animals: reference only the named cell.
3. Preserve recognisable anatomy, face, silhouette, covering and characteristic
   proportions. Vary pose, viewpoint, individual markings, light and setting
   when appropriate. A new scene is not permission to redesign a liked animal.
4. Put the animal into a believable moment. A field can have an existing grazer;
   a farmer's landscape can include local wildlife; canal, canopy, coast and
   settlement scenes can contain their own selected residents. They can be
   quiet background life, not always the central subject or a rules diagram.
5. For card proposals, record the animal code, exact reference and relevant
   ecology beside the idea. Genesis is an especially useful starting point,
   not an exclusive home for every animal. Habitat and context decide where
   wildlife appears. Card mechanics, domestication and human uses need their
   own justification; an animal near a farmer is not automatically livestock.
6. If nothing fits, develop a new proposal using the current art direction.
   Keep new biology separate from established facts and use lineup → selected
   reference → scene when developing a new species.

DESIGN LESSONS TO CARRY FORWARD
Weight, support, movement and feeding anatomy must make sense. Variety comes
from whole body plans, proportions and integrated fur, feathers, scales and
skin, not a familiar Earth animal with a new surface or a swapped face.
Reptilian and snake-like faces remain welcome within a broader range,
including mammalian structures. Do not redesign liked faces simply to vary
a batch. Do not repeat throat combs, jaw frills or a successful animal's
signature feature as the default novelty.

Aim for the ecological inventiveness, tactile life and surprising relationships
that inspired this exploration in Scavengers Reign, and the imposing unfamiliar
scale that prompted the Kenshi comparison. Create original animals. Giant
body plans have more freedom than the earlier small-animal heuristics, while
retaining coherent anatomy and presence; enormous does not have to mean horror.
Truly huge animals are rare and awe-inspiring, not routine background livestock.
Most animals simply live. They need not all be magical, useful to people,
symbiotic or exotic in every feature.

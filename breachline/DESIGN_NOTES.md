# Breachline design notes

A copy of the notes Claude keeps on this project, taken on 2026-11-05 so they travel with the repo. "I", "me" and "my" mean Claude, and "the user" means the game's creator. Claude's own memory stays on the computer it was written on, so on another computer ask Claude to read this file first.

Original card game (not a rebuild of Card Wars, Magic or anything else). Own names, art and cards. Working title Breachline is mine, not the user's. Prototype code is in the `breachline` folder of the Games project and is published as an artifact.

User's picks as of 2026-11-05: unseen summoner, one life total per player, 40-card deck built before play, reshuffle discards when deck empties, hand kept with one draw per turn, grid with movement, attacker picks target, targets strike back, damage stays until healed, new units wait a turn and appear only in the back row, units move 1 tile unless a keyword says otherwise, no diagonal movement (Swift can reach a diagonal tile with two steps), attack or ability, whole turns, limited instant responses (weaker than Magic's, some damage, some protection), hidden hand, some random effects, keywords plus short text, enchantments, 10-20 minute matches.

Stats: values have an added zero. Attack usually differs from health, with equal values uncommon. Average time to kill is 1.5-2.5 hits; strong units can one-shot and weak spearmen chew each other up quickly. Sunmane lancer should be a strong unit with good usable abilities.

Mana: soft coloured mana approved. Lands have colours, each card wants a number of lands of its colour, and each missing land adds 1 to the cost, so a card is never locked out. Lands sit off-grid below the board and can't be taken or destroyed. The per-turn card swap was removed on 2026-11-05 after playtesting: the user said it was way too powerful and made lands too easy, and prefers Magic's approach of redrawing only at the beginning. The prototype now uses a London-style mulligan (new full hand, then one card per redraw goes on the bottom). The limit of 2 redraws is my assumption. After that playtest the user asked for 5-card opening hands and a larger share of lands, and expects to move to larger decks later. The 20-card prototype decks now run 9 lands (45%; my pick from simulations, about 7% of players stuck on 2 or fewer lands at the start of their sixth turn, down from 19%). The user was fine with getting a land after a bad first hand and redrawing. In simulations with the scripted bot on both sides, the first player wins only about 40% and the white deck about 59%; neither has been addressed.

Town: sits on the side of the grid and takes up two tiles, so attackers reach it before the summoner and face a time tradeoff (get distracted or push on). It has little roads and three to four building slots. Each building is destroyed separately, which stops its boost. An attacker uses an arrow to aim at a specific building. The town can take a wall upgrade. Base rule is one building per colour; white has two for now as a reminder to build more. Faction cards are weak and look grayed out without their building; neutral cards have lower peak value and less variance. Bank stores unused mana.

Fortifications are buildings played onto the grid. Units with a matching keyword (giants, human archers) can garrison them for a defensive bonus; entering or exiting costs a turn. A wall should typically add about one turn of life, more only against very weak attackers or from a really good fortification. Fliers cross walls. User doesn't want this perfectly balanced.

First version: town and fortifications are not playable. The town gets an under-construction cover that looks like it's being built, and a falling-down wall sits at the side. This keeps early AI decisions simple. Faction cards play at full value until the town opens.

Modes: campaign is the main way to play; exhibition with a deck builder should also exist.

Alignments (decided 2026-11-05): five colours sit above factions as alignments, with several factions inside each, so it is not one faction per colour. Colour words are shorthand, and in-world names go on cards. General principles of Magic's five colours are the starting point, to adjust through discussion; the names need not match Magic. Decks are a main colour plus a splash. Rivalries are story only. User-picked features: white healing, protecting allies, standing together; red high attack with low health, direct damage, speed, giants and fliers; blue ranged, moving units, drawing cards, illusions and decoys, disabling enemies. Black and green are in, with features still to choose. The Tithebound was rejected as a main faction but could be a black/white hybrid later. Humans appear in all colours. User dislikes vampire flavour but likes the mechanics. Alignment names chosen: Vigil (white), Gnosis (blue), Dominion (black), Fervor (red, core idea is passion), Genesis (green). Strengths and weaknesses are strong tendencies, never absolutes: alignments can have rare exceptions (very few green fliers rather than none, some higher-health red units but fewer than white). About 1 card in 10 can go against its alignment's tendency, at a higher cost: usable and still reasonably efficient, but not ideal. Town buildings belong to factions, while lands and mana belong to alignments. Flavour text resumed on 2026-11-05 after the setting work. The user wants lines that are about the card's own character, end early, stay off the nose about the setting's themes, and vary in mood (hopeful is welcome; only puns and out-and-out jokes are out). Undefined place and people names are fine occasionally, to normalize later, but a batch with one on most lines was too many. Lands need little flavour text. Lines are tracked in `breachline/FLAVOUR.md`.

Art: the user wants a painted, detailed look with often very dynamic poses, similar in feel to Magic cards, generated by an outside image tool (ChatGPT or Codex). Briefs for the 19 current cards are in `breachline/art/ART_BRIEFS.md`, with images expected in the same folder. Proposed names: The Sunmane Oath, The Warhorde, The Lantern Conclave; lands Sunlit plains, Cinder peaks, Lantern quarter. The first card frame is built in `breachline/card.html` using the Sunmane lancer art, with title and cost at the top as the user asked. Flavour text preferences live in the project's `flavor-text` skill. As of 2026-11-05 all 19 images are in the prototype (hand cards, board tiles, large close-up), and the game loads the smaller copies in `breachline/art/web/`, so rerun `art/build-web.sh` after replacing any art. In the game the title bar is coloured by alignment (white gold, red crimson, blue navy), which differs from the crimson bar in `card.html`; the user hasn't confirmed that yet. The Games folder became a local git repo on 2026-11-05 (branch `main`, no remote), at the user's request; several threads share this checkout, so commit only your own files. First playtest with art, same day: the user found it fun, with a game that went back and forth and mana that eventually came up.

Setting (from the user, 2026-11-05; draft in `breachline/SETTING.md`, which marks my additions as Proposed): Age of Gods, when gods walked the world and then destroyed their bodies to form its foundation; Age of Empires, with one vast, god-ordained, generally benevolent Empire ("the Empire" or "the Old Empire") whose society needed the gods' direct intervention to survive; degeneration and factions; the Cataclysm (the user prefers that name to "the Unwinding"), when an intertwined, harmonious existence snapped and unwound like a rope pulling away, the gods stopped speaking and the Empire was destroyed almost entirely; the Silence, a brief dark age; now, about 150 years after the Cataclysm, new, mostly stable kingdoms with control undecided, and nobody alive remembers the Empire. The user rejected a drowned empire and a chain of islands: the world is Earth-like, with one large continent, some medium ones and several small ones. The present looks high fantasy, and the Bronze Age is only a historical comparison. A map may come later or may not, because it could limit role-playing. One campaign story per alignment. The world can definitely be saved, but what that means varies by alignment. Lands are gameplay only and don't matter to the setting.

Secret truth, known to nobody in the world: the gods are alive in another realm, separated by primordial chaos (the image is Satan crossing the void in Paradise Lost). The distance isn't infinite, but nobody knows if it can be crossed. The gods are diminished only because of that distance; close to their worshippers they grow strong. What snapped at the Cataclysm is undecided; the user's ideas all have the main god nearly destroyed, by sacrifice to keep the world from breaking, betrayal by another god, the Empire's evil technology or forbidden secrets, or cultists summoning a primordial entity. People variously believe the gods are dead, never existed, or are silent from disappointment or for benevolent reasons. Summoners descend from people touched in the Cataclysm; it works like a new gene across many bloodlines, not one family. Their power comes from a surviving thread into that realm, felt as a connection to chaos and its entities, to goodness, or to evil entities, but they only know they are different. Each has a golden, glowing, scar-like mark; the user wants both names in use, "the gilt scar" and "the goldmark", because not everyone in the world uses the same words for things. Art rule from the user: a summoner hiding the mark really hides it, so it is unseen or barely visible, never glowing through while they frantically cover it. Lesser gods remain, diminished, good or evil at the scale of a saint or a devil, each known by their own name on cards, and collectively called the Velunn, which the user chose because it allows slurs like the Veli or the Vels. They don't prove the gods exist; some think they are races left from the Age of Gods.

Alignments in the setting: Vigil restores the Empire and the gods, and suspects Gnosis of causing the fall through dark magic. Gnosis wants a new order and sees magic as science. Dominion wants ruin or a new dark order, and is the only alignment with no democratic streak. Fervor enjoys the freedom of chaos, with small Cossack-style democratic bands. Genesis wants small bands in nature, with an occasional eco-terrorist edge.

**Why:** Wants a game unique to them that an AI model (ideally Haiku, probably Sonnet) can play from the rules and general strategies, with a small on-screen chat showing its professed thoughts rather than its full reasoning.

**How to apply:** Planned order is diagrams, small decks and practice games (prototype done, scripted opponent only), then factions and the AI. Prototype assumptions not yet confirmed by the user: 5x4 grid, summoner hit only from enemy back row, 150 summoner life, only surviving targets strike back, play phase before command phase, first player skips first draw, two response windows with no stack.

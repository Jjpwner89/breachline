# Breachline setting (draft 1)

Everything here comes from the creator's premise unless it's marked **Proposed**. Proposed details are suggestions and haven't been approved.

## The world in brief

The gods built the world, gave up their bodies to make its foundation, and spoke to its people through a vast empire. Then the Cataclysm came, the gods fell silent, and the Empire collapsed into a dark age called the Silence. New kingdoms have since risen across an Earth-like world of continents. They are mostly stable, but nobody has settled who will rule or what the world should become.

The world can be saved. The alignments disagree about what saving it means, so each one's victory would look different.

## History

### The Age of Gods
Long ago, the gods walked among mortals and built the structures of the world. They then destroyed their physical forms to become the world's foundation. Some lesser gods stayed behind.

### The Age of Empires
The Empire, now also called the Old Empire, arose in this age, with the feel of the first great empires of the middle Bronze Age, but larger. The gods ordained it and still spoke to its people, so it was nearly all-powerful. It was generally benevolent, depending on who you ask, and its reign reached back beyond memory. Its society relied so heavily on the gods that it needed their direct intervention to survive.

### The Degeneration
The empire slowly declined, and factions formed with different views of what it should be.

### The Cataclysm
All of existence was once intertwined and harmonious, or that is the ideal people remember. Then something snapped, and everything unwound, like a rope rapidly pulling away. The gods stopped speaking and the Empire was destroyed almost entirely. Nobody in the world knows why.

### The Silence
With the gods gone, a society that depended on them could no longer survive as it was. A brief period of darkness and chaos followed, similar to the Bronze Age collapse.

### The present
New kingdoms have been established and are generally stable, with some cracks. Ultimate control is far from decided. About 150 years have passed since the cataclysm, and nobody alive remembers the old empire.

**Community character (creator direction, 2026-11-06):** this is a world emerging from a dark time, with established societies and the possibility of optimism. It is not a uniformly cosmopolitan world. Communities have distinct local populations and identities; ordinary scenes should not automatically mix every people and background. Lion folk generally keep to themselves. Orcs are not Vigil / white aligned. These are setting constraints, not a claim that every society is identical or that every individual lacks a distinct life.

## Look

The present day is high fantasy. The Bronze Age is only a historical comparison, not the look of the world.

## Magic

The source of magic is disputed:
- **Vigil** believes all magic comes from the gods, and that healing proves they are still present.
- **Gnosis** treats magic as a science, though some of its people have religious leanings.

Lands exist for gameplay only. They play no part in the setting's history or conflicts.

## Summoners

The Cataclysm touched some people, and the ability to summon has passed down through their descendants. It works less like one family's gift and more like a new gene entering the population, so it now runs through many bloodlines. Summoners know only that they are different.

Each summoner has a golden, glowing, scar-like mark somewhere on their body, called the gilt scar or the goldmark. Not everyone uses the same words for things in this world, and that variety is deliberate.

**Art rule:** some summoners hide the mark, and when they do, it really is hidden. In their art, it should be unseen or barely visible. Never show someone frantically covering a mark that still glows through.

**Proposed:** the trait doesn't pick a side, so summoners appear in every alignment.

## Lesser gods

Some lesser gods remain in the world, diminished, and they no longer speak for the high gods. They can be good or evil, but on the scale of a saint or a devil, not of God or Satan. Each one is known by their own name on cards, and as a group they are called the Velunn. People who dislike them use slurs such as the Veli or the Vels.

They are related to the gods, but their existence doesn't prove the gods are real. Some people think they are only races left over from the Age of Gods.

## Vampires

Vampires exist, but they are a subtle, rare part of the world and stay in the shadows. They can belong to Dominion or Fervor. They should never feel common or become a major presence on cards.

## Peoples, creatures and constructs

This catalogue brings the selected visual concepts into the setting notes at the creator's request on 2026-11-05. It records what each inhabitant looks like and how it might belong in the world. Working names can still change.

**Status:** existing inhabitants come from the established setting or current cards. **Included concept** means the creator requested that the concept be carried into these notes; detailed biology and culture remain open where noted. **Developing** entries need further design work. **Proposed** entries are new explorations awaiting review. An image's profession or alignment does not define an entire people. None of these entries settles the cause of the Cataclysm.

**Scale and rarity (creator direction, 2026-11-05):** truly enormous animals are rare. Their appearance should feel extraordinary and can cause people to stop in awe; they are not commonplace scenery. Ordinary and moderately large wildlife should remain available at believable scales.

### Included people: Alderkin

**Included concept.** Living tree people, with the first individual shown at roughly three metres tall. Their asymmetrical bodies grow as flexible trunks divided into rootlike legs, with branch arms and a few grasping root-fingers. The head grows naturally from the upper trunk; knot-like eyes and posture convey attention without a detailed human face carved into bark.

The first scene shows an alderkin clearing an old irrigation channel while a human plants saplings. This suggests cooperation and stewardship, but gardening is not a universal occupation or an obligatory Genesis allegiance. They have their own lives and intentions and do not always help humans; the Waterkeeper scene does not establish a general irrigation or service role. Their origins, reproduction, lifespan and relationship to walking saplings remain undecided.

**Proposed development:** an older alderkin can use flexible branches to shelter a traveller in winter. The creator likes the winter tree-person design; seasonal appearance and this behaviour are still being explored, not a rule for all alderkin.

Art: [Waterkeeper](art/concepts/alderkin-waterkeeper.png), [Winter shelter](art/concepts/alderkin-winter-shelter.png).

### Developing people: Stonehorn

**Developing.** An ibex-inspired people with genuinely caprine faces, swept-back horns, grasping hands and sure-footed cloven lower legs. The bridgekeeper wears practical clothes and repairs a crossing, suggesting a person with a place in everyday society.

The creator liked this design least in the first creature batch. Retain the entry as a working option to revisit rather than treating its current appearance as settled. That response is not a general rejection of animal-inspired peoples. No universal mountain culture, profession or alignment has been established.

Art: [Bridgekeeper](art/concepts/stonehorn-bridgekeeper.png).

### Included animal: Coalback

**Included concept; strong favourite.** A small, heat-tolerant, burrowing animal about forty centimetres long. It has a supple body, a softly furred face, strong digging forepaws, a thick tail and broad rust-coloured keratin plates over its back. Its compact silhouette should feel invented but biologically coherent.

The observed behaviour is gathering warm charcoal from the edge of a travellers' cookfire. **Proposed ecology:** coalbacks carry residual warmth into insulated nests, bringing them into ordinary contact with camps and settlements. Their heat tolerance is not unlimited, and the concept does not require fire-breathing or spectacular magic.

Art: [Coalback forager](art/concepts/coalback-forager.png).

### Included animal: Reedcrown

**Included concept.** A pony-sized amphibious grazer with a compact body, long flexible neck, soft muzzle, four broad webbed feet and a tapering muscular tail. Two thin, flexible crests behind the head give it a recognisable silhouette and catch transmitted light.

It grazes on riverweed in shallow water. The first scene places it alongside a distant ferry, suggesting wildlife sharing travelled waterways rather than an isolated monster encounter. **Proposed biology:** its crests are sensory structures; their exact function, distribution and any domestication remain open.

Art: [Reedcrown grazer](art/concepts/reedcrown-grazer.png).

### Included animal: Dawnsail

**Included concept; strongest favourite of the creature batch.** An immense whale-like aerial animal with a gently bowed pale underside, small forward sensory lobes, a long tapering tail and two great translucent sail-fins supported by a few graceful ribs. Its scale can rival a great hall or cathedral. Light passing through the sails is a key part of its visual identity.

A small observer in a grand landscape makes its scale and presence meaningful. Dawnsails are living creatures, not automatically gods or Velunn. Their method of flight, diet, intelligence, origin and relationship to the Silence remain undecided.

**Proposed development:** a few dawnsails pass over cultivated valleys at blue hour. This tests migration and coexistence with ordinary farming life; it does not establish a fixed route or map. The creator likes the people pausing in awe: such a sight is exceptional, not routine. For anatomy, the FIRST coastal-passage painting remains the reference; the harvest painting drifts too far from that original design.

Art: [Coastal passage](art/concepts/dawnsail-passage.png), [Over the harvest](art/concepts/dawnsails-over-the-harvest.png).

### Proposed animal: Raincup

**Proposed.** A hand-sized dry-country animal with four padded feet, a balancing tail and supple skin frills that fold into a cup behind its head. It tilts the frills to direct gathered dew toward its mouth. Pale jade membranes, sandy skin and this small purposeful behaviour distinguish it from a flower-shaped ornament or a magical familiar.

The proposed ecological role is a dawn-active animal along dry caravan routes. Its physiology and range need review.

Art: [Raincup at first light](art/concepts/raincup-at-first-light.png).

### Proposed animal: Slateprowler

**Proposed.** A wolf-sized cliff predator with a low flexible body, four gripping limbs, a broad flattened head, small amber eyes and a balancing tail with a widened tip. Its smooth blue-black hide and ability to lie close to stone make the silhouette quiet and unfamiliar.

The first scene shows it descending a rock face, using its tail and feet to place its weight carefully. Danger comes from patient attention and movement, not gore or a monstrous face. Habitat, prey and social behaviour remain open.

Art: [On the descent](art/concepts/slateprowler-on-the-descent.png).

### Proposed animal: Tidevault

**Proposed.** A large shallow-water creature with a low body beneath a broad fluted shell, forming two connected natural vaults. Stout jointed walking limbs carry it through the surf, while shorter forelimbs gather food from the shallows. The shell is living material, not a ruin or city on an animal's back.

**Proposed relationship:** its passage stirs coastal water, and fishers may learn to work safely around its wake. The old-harbour scene tests that relationship. The creator found the old-harbour painting too large; future designs should reduce its scale. Size limits, feeding habits and temperament are not fixed.

Art: [At the old harbour](art/concepts/tidevault-at-the-old-harbour.png).

### Proposed construct model: Spindlejack

**Proposed Artificer design.** A compact repair construct with an open spool-shaped body, a visible thread reel, four bracing legs, two fine manipulators and a small shaded glass sensor. Brass ribs and sparse enamel panels leave functional negative spaces. It is a made tool or being, not a new biological people.

The first scene shows one repairing a ferry sail while a human holds the torn edges steady. Its useful action should explain its anatomy. The creator strongly likes this compact sewing construct and its appealing design. Whether all such constructs are self-directed or conscious remains undecided.

Art: [Mending the sail](art/concepts/spindlejack-mending-the-sail.png).

## What people believe about the gods

People disagree about why the gods went quiet:
- They are dead.
- They never existed.
- They still exist and are silent out of disappointment.
- They still exist and are silent for benevolent reasons.

## The truth about the gods

This is for the creator only, and no one in the world knows it.

The gods are alive, in another realm. The rope that pulled away at the Cataclysm is really a great distance in an interdimensional sense, and primordial chaos now fills the gap. The image is Satan crossing the void from Hell in *Paradise Lost* and seeing Heaven in the distance. The distance isn't infinite, but crossing it would take such strength that nobody knows if it can be done.

The gods are diminished only because they are so far away. Close to their worshippers, they grow strong.

Summoners draw their power from this. The thread that once bound them to the gods is somehow still connected, and it really leads into that primordial realm. A summoner might feel a connection to the swirling chaos and the entities within it, to goodness, or to evil entities directly. None of them knows this is what they are feeling.

**Canonical visual reference (2026-11-06):** [The Unmeasured Distance](art/campaign-2026-11-05/20-experiment-unmeasured-distance.png), the gold strand crossing blue and magenta space, is approved by the creator as canon. It depicts the surviving connection and immense separation described here. This approval does not settle the cause of the Cataclysm, establish a successful crossing, or give characters knowledge they do not have.

### What snapped

This isn't decided yet. The creator's ideas so far centre on the main god being nearly destroyed and heavily diminished:
- He gave himself as a sacrifice to keep the world from breaking.
- Another god betrayed him.
- The Empire developed evil technology or forbidden secrets that destroyed him.
- Cultists summoned a great primordial entity that destroyed him.

## The alignments

Alignments are outlooks and don't map neatly onto states or factions. Hybrid cards can come later. Humans appear in all of them.

### Vigil (white)
- **Wants:** to restore the old empire and bring back the gods.
- **Believes:** the way was lost when people stopped revering the gods. It senses the gods' presence through healing magic, or at least believes it does.
- **Politics:** may favour a broad, hegemonic form of democracy, among other forms.
- **Suspects:** that Gnosis helped cause the fall by studying dark magic. This is suspicion, not outright hatred.

### Gnosis (blue)
- **Wants:** a new order. It is not opposed to organised society, but it has no interest in rebuilding the old empire.
- **Believes:** the empire stifled innovation and had stopped moving forward. Magic is a science.

### Dominion (black)
- **Environment (creator correction, 2026-11-06):** substantially harsher and worse than the benign, orderly city in the rejected campaign painting, and less cosmopolitan. Its dark order must not read as merely a tidy civic alternative.
- **Wants:** either for the degeneration to continue into utter ruin, or to build a new, dark order.
- **Politics:** the one alignment with no democratic streak.

### Fervor (red)
- **Wants:** to keep the freedom it has found in the chaos, and to prevent the empire's return.
- **Politics:** prefers smaller bands with democratic streaks, closer to a Cossack host than to a modern state.

### Genesis (green)
- **Wants:** to live in nature, in small bands, with no return to the empire.
- **Believes:** Gnosis would destroy the land for industry, Fervor is destructive, and Vigil is on a fool's errand.
- **At its extreme:** some of its people act as eco-terrorists.

## What each alignment's saved world looks like

**Proposed:** one idea for each alignment's campaign ending.

- **Vigil:** the empire is restored and the gods answer, at a cost that may be too much to bear.
- **Gnosis:** a new society that no longer needs the gods to answer.
- **Dominion:** peace under one dark order, or the end of everything that could suffer.
- **Fervor:** no throne ever again, and free bands across the land.
- **Genesis:** the ruins are overgrown, and people live within the wild.

## The game's shape

- **World:** Earth-like, with one large continent, some medium ones and several small ones.
- **Campaign:** one story for each alignment.
- **Map:** a campaign map is wanted; the current game has a route map. Both painted world/region studies from the 2026-11-05 art batch were rejected on 2026-11-06 for unconvincing scale. Their geography and place assignments are not canon. Keep wider geography loose while a suitable map direction is developed.

## Open questions

1. **Names:** the gods and the continents still need names. They can be longer than one word.
2. **The snap:** which of the ideas above, if any, is the true cause of the Cataclysm?
3. **Title:** Breachline is still a working title, and nothing in the setting explains it yet.

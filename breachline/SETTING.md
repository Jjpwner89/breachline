# Breachline setting (draft 1)

Everything here comes from the creator's premise unless it's marked **Proposed**. Proposed details are suggestions and haven't been approved.

## The world in brief

The gods built the world, gave up their bodies to make its foundation, and spoke to its people through a vast empire. Then the Cataclysm came, the gods fell silent, and the Empire collapsed into a dark age called the Silence. New kingdoms have since risen across an Earth-like world of continents. They are mostly stable, but nobody has settled who will rule or what the world should become.

The world can be saved. The alignments disagree about what saving it means, so each one's victory would look different. These are hard times, and hope is real but not easy: the creator put hope at 2 out of 5.

## History

### The Age of Gods
Long ago, the gods walked among mortals and built the structures of the world. They then destroyed their physical forms to become the world's foundation. Some lesser gods stayed behind.

### The Age of Empires
The Empire, now also called the Old Empire, arose in this age, with the feel of the first great empires of the middle Bronze Age, but larger. The gods ordained it and still spoke to its people, so it was nearly all-powerful. It was generally benevolent, depending on who you ask, and its reign reached back beyond memory. Its society relied so heavily on the gods that it needed their direct intervention to survive.

### The Degeneration
The empire slowly declined, and factions formed with different views of what it should be.

### The Cataclysm
All of existence was once intertwined and harmonious, or that is the ideal people remember. Then something snapped, and everything unwound, like a rope rapidly pulling away. The gods stopped speaking and the Empire was destroyed almost entirely. Nobody in the world knows why.

### The Silence
With the gods gone, a society that depended on them could no longer survive as it was. A brief period of darkness and chaos followed, similar to the Bronze Age collapse.

### The present
New kingdoms have been established and are generally stable, with some cracks. Ultimate control is far from decided. About 150 years have passed since the cataclysm, and no mortal alive remembers the old empire. Long-lived beings such as the Velunn or dragons may.

**Communities:** this is a world emerging from a dark time, with established societies and the possibility of optimism. Most places are not cosmopolitan. Communities have their own local populations and identities, and some mixing is fine. Where different peoples do meet, their differences matter and are part of the setting. Lion folk generally keep to themselves. Orcs are never Vigil.

### Scale and uneven capability (creator direction, 2026-11-09)

Keep the setting large and varied. Great cities can coexist with smaller settlements, rural communities and less capable regions. Magic can compensate for inefficiencies in industry, enabling selected achievements that would otherwise seem out of time, including robots or constructs. Their existence does not require a uniformly modern industrial economy or equal capabilities everywhere.

**Exploratory Ambrel direction:** the creator likes the city backdrop from the Cobalt duelist study as a possible larger-scale Ambrel. Its tall urban fabric, bridges, glazed arcade and magical-city character are visual references; the completed city image is not a settled street plan or map. Reference: [Ambrel city direction](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/design-directions-round-06-2026-11-08/10b-ambrel-city-preserved.png).

**Tone under exploration:** the creator is interested in greater present-day capability, potentially a world less dominated by the immediate consequences of the old catastrophe and more threatened by powerful forces ahead. Dominion was raised as a possible world-threatening power. Develop these implications gently; the balance of recovery, danger and optimism remains open. The established Cataclysm and approximately 150-year interval remain unchanged by this visual exploration.

### Ambrel and the surviving university (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Ambrel is a centre of learning and magic. Its university survived the fall of the Empire, preserving a concentration of teaching, practitioners and refined magical technique. Magic is learned, and its practice is especially developed and optimized here. Mages and magical tinkerers also work elsewhere; Ambrel concentrates them rather than monopolizing magic.

The creator compares this advantage to a society where broader education makes minor practical magic more accessible. This is an analogy for the relationship between learning, capability and prosperity, not a one-to-one equation between literacy and magical power. Personal aptitude still matters. Magical industry need not spread in the same way as ordinary industry, and Ambrel's advantages do not make every resident a mage or give every other settlement the same capabilities.

**Practical magical education (creator direction, 2026-11-13):** Ambrel teaches magic as a practical trade as well as a scholarly discipline. The creator retained this worldbuilding idea while rejecting the Lessons of Brass painting; no specific school, curriculum or pictured apparatus is established by it. Learning still depends on aptitude and training.

### Imperial remnants (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Surviving imperial infrastructure is mostly ordinary remnants: roads, buildings and similar works. This is the baseline for the Empire's physical legacy, rather than widespread remarkable systems still operating beyond present understanding. Exceptional relics remain possible, but are not established by this direction.

### Distant places and surviving knowledge (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Distant oceans and remote regions can contain creatures and places that present-day educated people regard as sailors' stories. The Empire knew more of these things. Some of that knowledge may survive in an obscure book in Ambrel, but it is not common knowledge now. A surviving record can exist without its contents being widely known or accepted.

This leaves room for both exploration and rediscovery: an encounter may reveal something once documented, while the relevant text remains little known. A specific book, expedition or creature is not established merely by this general direction.

## Look

The present day is high fantasy. The Bronze Age is only a historical comparison, not the look of the world.

Visual production follows the [current art direction](../.claude/skills/art-direction/SKILL.md) and [accepted reference guidance](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/README.txt). The approved round-07 work supports ordinary varied faces, material-specific reflection and deliberately varied painted detail. Constructs can use abstract functional anatomy; selected Asian armor details can blend with established kit, and modest curved eaves are a valid architectural option. These are visual possibilities, not mandatory styles or new universal world rules.

**Adult tone (creator direction, 2026-11-09):** The setting has room for more visible adult attraction, flirtation, sensual dress and in-universe glamour or pinup art. The creator wants selected adult characters with fuller busts and visible cleavage as part of a broader range of bodies and presentation. The first variants are collected in art/nsfw; their individual designs and portrait situations remain exploratory until selected.

## Magic

The source of magic is disputed:
- **Vigil** believes all magic comes from the gods, and that healing proves they are still present.
- **Gnosis** treats magic as a science, though some of its people have religious leanings.

Lands exist for gameplay only. They play no part in the setting's history or conflicts.

### Attunement, learning and personal energy (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Magic is present but its skilled use is not very common. The creator's comparison is reading in the Middle Ages, with a stronger inherited component: learning matters, and so does natural aptitude. Most people can feel magic in some way. Sensing it and being able to use it well are different capabilities.

A person with normal attunement could technically learn a small, ordinary spell, but for most people even this would be very hard. Broad sensitivity to magic therefore coexists with uncommon practical spellcasting. The creator compares this gap between theoretical learnability and practical accomplishment to a highly demanding intellectual discipline. The illustrative “1%” is not a fixed population statistic or a universal intelligence-to-magical-power formula.

Magical practice requires a connection to a non-physical magical plane. The muscle analogy extends to this connection: training develops a capability on that plane. Different beings and orders have different cadences or ways of developing it. This does not yet define the plane's relationship to the gods' realm or settle the disputed ultimate source of magic.

Formal education is the prototypical learning route, with family traditions, apprenticeships, teachers and religious orders also having a place. Vigil and, to some extent, Dominion can teach through religious orders. Self-taught practitioners exist but are less common; learning is not chiefly unguided experimentation. Natural talent can be evident early in childhood, while training still develops capacity and efficiency.

Humans are generally normally attuned. **Tentative:** some species may be unattuned, probably rarely; elves might be more attuned and goblins a little less. These are possible tendencies, not fixed species rankings, numerical modifiers or limits on particular individuals.

**Working model:** people have individual levels of magical energy or capacity. The comparison is muscular strength, but with much wider variation between individuals; the creator's “10–20× as much variation” is a rough sense of scale rather than a fixed numerical power distribution. Learning and technique matter alongside this capacity. **Training increases both capacity and efficiency.** Food and rest replenish personal magical energy in part. External magic can also be drawn on; the creator's latest practical examples emphasize channeling from a magical stone, an amulet, a wand or a stone set in a wand. A person need not first absorb all that energy into their own reserve. The exact relationship between absorption, replenishment and immediate channeling, and their rates, remains open.

Using magic entails exertion, like using muscles. Severe overexertion or exhausting one's magical energy can be fatal.

**Typical exhaustion progression:** slight loss of control, then difficulty concentrating, then physical fatigue, then near-complete loss of control. This is a typical progression, not an inevitable sequence for every practitioner. A highly trained mage can maintain control as exhaustion develops, like an athlete maintaining form as their body tires. Training can preserve technique under strain without removing the underlying exertion or the danger of overuse. Exact injury thresholds and recovery times remain open; exhaustion has no mandatory visual effect.

**When control fails:** spells most often fizzle or go astray, but dangerously unpredictable outcomes are also possible. The danger depends in part on the individual's latent power and training. Someone with substantial raw power and little training can become dangerous when control slips; a person with less raw magical aptitude is more likely to have their spell simply fizzle out. These are typical tendencies rather than a fixed outcome for every failed spell.

### Training different kinds of magic (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

The creator's preferred working analogy is different muscles: different kinds of magic can be exercised, and someone can become good at several or all of them. The variation is more extreme than ordinary muscle training. Scholarly technique and more instinctual kinds of magic both have room in the world.

**Exploratory:** alignment, emotional disposition, behaviour and magical affinity may reinforce one another. The creator considered intelligence and moral alignment as explanations, then favoured the different-muscles analogy; neither a direct intelligence-to-power rule nor a universal evilness-to-power rule is established. Soul absorption was raised as a possible way to gain Dominion power, not as a required act or a settled general mechanism. Preserve the emotional and personal dimension while leaving the precise relationship to alignment and nurture open. This discussion does not settle the disputed ultimate source of magic or make divine approval a requirement.

### Magical energy and industry (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Magical energy powers magical machinery. Some magical energy also exists in the world, and magical beings create or sustain varying amounts, often small. Some industries rely on imbued items, charged by mages or using magical stones and materials. A practitioner need not stand beside a device continually feeding it.

**Enchanted possessions are a significant luxury.** The existence of magical industry does not make personally owned enchanted equipment commonplace.

**Using enchanted items:** ordinary people can use enchanted objects without being skilled spellcasters. A magical key or an enhanced sword can be usable by almost anyone. How well someone wields an item, and whether they can control a strongly enchanted one, can depend on their ability. An exceptionally powerful staff may be difficult even for a less powerful mage to control properly. Requirements vary with the item; basic use does not imply mastery of its capabilities.

**Enchantment duration and fuel:** these vary by item. Some enchantments persist indefinitely, such as a permanently enhanced blade; other items consume stored energy and need replenishing, such as a staff using a finite reserve. These are possible designs, not universal rules for all blades or staves. An item's energy source and replenishment method should be established for that item. Replenishing an item does not make the Mountain's Lung's permanently spent mana-stones rechargeable.

**Enduring enchanted places (creator direction, 2026-11-10):** rare places can retain an enchantment for years without an active caster maintaining it. This establishes enduring place enchantments as possible, while leaving an individual site's origin, energy source and limits open.

Living beings generally convert or channel energy into magical work more efficiently than machines. Efficiency is distinct from total output: a large material-fuelled device can still provide more power than a person. The creator's fuel and wattage comparisons describe this shared capacity to do work, not literal coal-to-magic chemistry or fixed conversion rates. Magical energy can support heat and fire, movement, transformation and other workings; it is not exclusively heat or industrial fuel.

**The Mountain's Lung burns stored mana from stones to provide energy.** This is the creator's explanation of the selected engine painting. **After use, these mana-stones are permanently spent.** Their material appearance afterward and their origin remain open; do not turn them into rechargeable batteries. This established example does not require all machinery to use the same source.

Mana-stones are accessible. **Tentative supply influence (creator direction, 2026-11-10):** Ambrel may try to exert some control over them. The extent and means of that control remain undecided; exclusive ownership or a complete monopoly has not been established.

Art: [The Mountain's Lung](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/concept-round-08-2026-11-09/11-the-mountains-lung.png).

Stored mana in the fiction is distinct from the game's mana rules. This lore does not introduce a player mana bank, a building system or a new card type. Its gameplay effect belongs in the card-design records.

### Dangerous magic and enforcement (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Harmful uses of magic are mostly treated like other violence. Special enforcement may be needed to pursue particularly powerful rogue mages; its institutions and methods are not yet defined. Individuals in this world can have more extraordinary capabilities than people in ours. That scope for exceptional personal power does not establish unlimited magic or immunity from consequences.

### Portals (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Portals are possible but generally rare because they require **unusual talent and difficult learned technique**. Their range, energy demands and destination requirements remain open; high energy costs or prepared endpoints have not been established as the reason for their rarity. An approved portal painting establishes an instance, not a widespread transport network.

### Healing, revival and spirits (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Healing has substantial capabilities but real limits. The creator's comparison is modern medicine: much can be done, yet people still die despite treatment. Healing does not make death inconsequential.

**Revival and soul loss:** when someone dies, their soul begins to leak away. An almost immediate revival can leave them mostly intact, and an exceptionally skilled healer may restore some lost soul. Time since death matters: the creator expects revival after a day or several days to leave the person diminished.

That loss can affect memory, emotion, independence and bodily integrity or function, in varying degrees. Someone may still be able to participate in life while living with lasting changes. The creator compares that changed functioning to the effects of a traumatic brain injury; this is a character-development analogy, not a fixed set of symptoms for every revived person.

**Soul and body:** they are separate but related, and the body contains some portion of the soul. Bodily recovery and soul recovery are connected aspects of healing; restoring one does not establish that the other is whole. Lost soul can be partially regained, with scarring possible after healing. The exact forms and limits of that scarring remain open. A zombie has both a partial soul and a partial body.

The prospect of a diminished return explains the different approaches to revival. Vigil distinguishes that from immediate rescue: it would attempt an immediate revival, but only if the healer were strong enough to perform it.

| Alignment | Approach to diminished revival |
| --- | --- |
| Vigil | Would attempt an immediate revival only if the healer were sufficiently strong. Values the soul and otherwise prefers to let it rest rather than bring the person back diminished. |
| Gnosis | Experiments with soul magic. **Tentative Ambrel direction:** restoring someone and using magical energy to sustain them. This is an experimental possibility, not established routine treatment. |
| Dominion | Accepts a diminished return. It would not generally bother adding magical energy to sustain fuller functioning, but may add energy to imbue strength. |
| Fervor | Would probably not bother with revival; this is a tendency, not an absolute prohibition. |
| Genesis | Wants death to reclaim the body. |

Exact time thresholds, how far soul restoration can go after a longer delay, the requirements for repairing fatal bodily damage and the other costs of revival remain open. Soul leakage has no established visual form. The possibility of magical energy sustaining a restored person does not establish that every revived person needs an ongoing external supply.

Spirits can be conscious, but are generally distant, weak, less present or diffuse. Think of low magical energy or a weaker presence in the world; do not assume every spirit is a fully vigorous person who merely happens to be transparent. Individual appearances and degrees of presence can vary.

### Working boundaries of magic and craft (creator direction, 2026-11-08)

Magic is not inherently dependent on physical equipment. People and creatures can wield it, and items can also be imbued with magical energy that users harness. No single method is the default for all magic; a visible caster directly feeding an effect or machine is not required.

Magic and industry can meet in particular crafts and devices, but they are not inseparable throughout the setting. The accepted field loom and harmonic wardwright are positive examples, not a universal production system or equipment standard.

For aerial travel, the creator prefers flying creatures or explicitly magical flight to mechanical/magitech hovering vessels. The floating freight barge concept is rejected; detailed flight abilities and their prevalence remain open.

**Under review:** a large walking crane driven by steam, possibly with magic supplying heat or power for the steam. The exact magical input, operating limits and prevalence are not established.

## Summoners

The Cataclysm touched some people, and the ability to summon has passed down through their descendants. It works less like one family's gift and more like a new gene entering the population, so it now runs through many bloodlines. Summoners know only that they are different.

Each summoner has a golden, glowing, scar-like mark somewhere on their body, called the gilt scar or the goldmark. Not everyone uses the same words for things in this world, and that variety is deliberate.

**Art rule:** some summoners hide the mark, and when they do, it really is hidden. In their art, it should be unseen or barely visible. Never show someone frantically covering a mark that still glows through.

**Proposed:** the trait doesn't pick a side, so summoners appear in every alignment.

## Lesser gods

Some lesser gods remain in the world, diminished, and they no longer speak for the high gods. They can be good or evil, but on the scale of a saint or a devil, not of God or Satan. Each one is known by their own name on cards, and as a group they are called the Velunn. People who dislike them use slurs such as the Veli or the Vels.

They are related to the gods, but their existence doesn't prove the gods are real. Some people think they are only races left over from the Age of Gods.

## Vampires

Vampires exist, but they are a subtle, rare part of the world and stay in the shadows. They can belong to Dominion or Fervor.

### Dragon intelligence and communication (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Dragons are certainly intelligent while remaining deeply primal beings. Communication is possible through a connection on a magical or non-physical level; someone must establish that connection rather than assume a dragon can simply be addressed and understood. A successful connection can carry complex ideas, but communicating them is difficult. Intelligence does not make their minds human-like or their behaviour tame. Ordinary speech, the method of connection and whether any bond persists are not established. This direction concerns dragons; the capabilities of all drakes are not yet settled.

### Construct behaviour (creator direction, 2026-11-10)

Most constructs follow narrow instructions. Adaptability is possible but rare. A construct can have some personality while remaining limited to those instructions and without being conscious; personality, adaptability and consciousness are distinct qualities.

The most advanced constructs can be meaningful to a human, while still functioning and relating in ways distinctly unlike a human mind. Do not assume human-equivalent understanding or personhood from an appealing personality. Whether any construct is truly conscious, and the precise limits of the most advanced examples, remain open.

### Included people: Alderkin

**Included concept.** Living tree people, with the first individual shown at roughly three metres tall. Their asymmetrical bodies grow as flexible trunks divided into rootlike legs, with branch arms and a few grasping root-fingers. The head grows naturally from the upper trunk; knot-like eyes and posture convey attention without a detailed human face carved into bark.

The first scene shows an alderkin clearing an old irrigation channel while a human plants saplings. This suggests cooperation and stewardship, but gardening is not a universal occupation or an obligatory Genesis allegiance. They have their own lives and intentions and do not always help humans; the Waterkeeper scene does not establish a general irrigation or service role. Their origins, reproduction, lifespan and relationship to walking saplings remain undecided.

**Proposed development:** an older alderkin can use flexible branches to shelter a traveller in winter. The creator likes the winter tree-person design; seasonal appearance and this behaviour are still being explored, not a rule for all alderkin.

Art: [Waterkeeper](art/concepts/alderkin-waterkeeper.png), [Winter shelter](art/concepts/alderkin-winter-shelter.png).

### Included people: Riverfolk (creator direction, 2026-11-13)

**Included concept; working name.** A people of human proportions with fin-like ears, gill folds along the neck and patches of scale on the skin. They are at home in water and on land.

- **Alignments:** split between Gnosis and Genesis. In Ambrel and other Gnosis places they work on the waterworks. They dive to clear and repair canals, sluices and channels, and some of them do hydrology and charting. Elsewhere they live in marsh and riverside villages closer to Genesis ways of life.
- **Standing:** generally in a less privileged place in society. Their skills are needed, but they rarely hold the better positions.
- **Numbers and places:** not very rare, but there is no riverfolk city or even a large town. They live in small villages, or in small numbers on the edges of cities, usually near the water.
- **Not:** they don't dive for mana-stones. Their diving is maintenance of canals and similar works.

Their culture, faith and history beyond this remain open.

Art: [Exploration sheet](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/active-scenes-and-people-2026-11-11/12-riverfolk-exploration.png).

### The new peoples (creator direction, 2026-11-13)

The creator approved this lore on 2026-11-13, building on Codex's lore handoff (`PEOPLES_LORE_HANDOFF.txt`). Each new people is local, like the Riverfolk: they have a home region and appear only in small numbers anywhere else, which keeps most places from being cosmopolitan. Each entry gives alignments, standing, numbers and places, and a **Not** line. Any alignment or job shown on a sample character belongs to that person, not the whole people. Names are working names unless marked otherwise.

### Included people: Kitefolk (final visual design and lore, 2026-11-13)

**Accepted visual design; working name.** KR1 is the creator's final Kitefolk reference. It shows slender, long-limbed people with mottled gliding membranes supported by elongated outer fingers, sharply angular faces and a long pointed nose. The pictured individual's age, clothing and hair are examples, not universal traits.

- **Home:** high mountain villages built at cliff edges, where a launch is a short walk from the door.
- **Alignments:** mostly Vigil, around high shrines and the old imperial passes, and Fervor, in free mountain bands.
- **Gliding:** they glide but can't fly. They need height and wind, and they walk back up. Gliding is how they travel, not how they fight.
- **Work:** courier is one common job; most Kitefolk herd and farm terraces.
- **Not:** fliers, or a people of messengers.
- **Village life:** small clan villages where each family keeps its own launch ledge. A child's first long glide marks growing up.
- **Conflict:** Vigil Kitefolk guard the high shrines while Fervor Kitefolk bands charge tolls on the same passes, so one people stands on both sides of a road.
- **Magic:** no special gift. Their wind-working is a learned tradition, not biology.
- **Individuals:** some can't glide well, from injury or build, and live below. Others leave for the lowlands.
- **Story:** keep them out of the current campaign stories (creator, 2026-11-13).

Art: [Final Kitefolk reference](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/peoples-final-2026-11-13/01-kitefolk-final.png).

### Included people: Glimmerfolk (final visual design and lore, 2026-11-13)

**Accepted visual design; working name.** GR1 is the creator's final Glimmerfolk reference. It shows very slender bodies, long necks, broad soft ear-fans and blue luminous freckles on matte dark skin; dotted lines along the arms spread into denser clusters toward wrists and hands. The eyes don't glow.

- **Home:** the undercity, the old city that a newer one was built over, the way Paris sits on its catacombs. They live in certain districts of Ambrel and of a few other cities. Their undercity is developed and decent, not a place to hide in caves.
- **Alignments:** mostly Gnosis, with some Genesis in wild caves.
- **Standing:** they live a little apart, but they aren't looked down on the way the Riverfolk are. Most people are at ease meeting one.
- **Numbers:** they live long lives and have few children, so there are far fewer of them than humans and not many are seen around.
- **Community:** tight-knit and fairly conservative. Some of the young are precocious and restless.
- **Conflict:** at the undercity's edges, where it gives way to sewers and stranger, unsafe places underground. They have no quarrel with the city above.
- **Built for the dark:** their glow and their very sharp hearing both come from living underground, and together they make Glimmerfolk excellent at finding their way through it.
- **The glimmers:** brighten and dim with mood, and they can signal to each other by light in the dark. This is their biology, not a spell. Every pattern is unique, and Glimmerfolk recognise each other by it.
- **Magic:** they have some attunement, and some study at Ambrel's university. Their glimmers flare when they cast, so a Glimmerfolk mage can't hide their spellcasting.
- **Not:** a sound-magic people. Their hearing is biology, so it shouldn't become a way around Gnosis's limit on sound cards.
- **Glimmer double:** the Lantern Conclave spell keeps its name. The creator isn't worried about it implying anything about Glimmerfolk.

Art: [Final Glimmerfolk reference](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/peoples-final-2026-11-13/02-glimmerfolk-final.png).

### Included people: Moor folk (final visual design and lore, 2026-11-13)

**Accepted visual design; working name.** The creator finalized PL7, the six-variation Moor folk sheet. ### Included people: the Ashborn (final visual design and lore, 2026-11-13)

**Accepted visual design; name chosen by the creator.** The Ashborn are the people earlier called the made people. The creator finalized PL3D, the original six-person raised-dead lineup, before the added-scarring revision. Preserve that sheet unchanged: ordinary adult scale, lean or average bodies, cold pallor, hollow facial planes, diminished gazes and restrained old healed scars. The whole visual range is accepted; no individual was singled out. PL3E's stronger scarring went too far.

- **What they are:** Dominion's dead, raised long after death and given magical energy for strength. They are made from the dead, not born. This replaces the earlier idea of a people bred by the Old Empire. Dominion's method traces back to forbidden imperial research.
- **Body and soul:** their bodies are fully repaired, apart from old scars, and only the soul is diminished. That makes them more substantial than the Gravebound's zombies, which are partial in both body and soul.
- **Soul:** a weak soul, not no soul. The setting already says that even a zombie keeps part of its soul. How much is lost can differ from person to person.
- **Who they were:** anyone Dominion can claim, including its own soldiers, executed prisoners and village dead. Dominion doesn't ask.
- **Numbers:** they can't have children, so there are only as many as Dominion makes.
- **Drawing life:** some Ashborn are made so they can draw life force from others, in a vampiric way. This isn't common, and most Ashborn are in a wretched position, but such an Ashborn can steal life. It's one of the many ways Dominion spends the souls and life it tithes.
- **Standing:** usually low. Dominion is cruel to its own, and the Ashborn are spent like any other resource. Very rarely one rises to real power, the way some enslaved people rose high in the Ottoman Empire, but nobody would choose to be Ashborn.
- **Life:** they have no community of their own. They're kept in barracks and listed in ledgers as the Court's property. Some keep scraps of their old lives, such as a habit or a name.
- **How much survives:** depends on how long they were dead before being raised. Raised early, they're mostly themselves; raised late, they're nearly blank.
- **Conflict:** families under Dominion sometimes recognise their dead among the Ashborn. Vigil and Genesis both see them as dead who should be put to rest: Vigil because souls should rest, Genesis because death should reclaim the body.
- **Magic:** a weak soul makes ordinary spellcasting hard. Their power is whatever Dominion put into them.
- **Alignment:** Dominion only, always. They serve the Ashen Court. Outside Dominion, people treat them as walking dead.
- **Not:** stitched laboratory constructs or giant ogres.
- **Name:** Ashborn (creator, 2026-11-13), chosen over Graftborn, Afterborn, Twiceborn, the Returned and the Mustered.

Art: [Final made-people visual range](art/reference-library-2026-11-06/peoples-final-2026-11-13/04-made-people-final-lineup.png).

### Included people: Banded folk, salt and silt variants (final visual design and lore, 2026-11-13)

**Accepted final visual design and lore.** One species with two variants. The shared brow, cheek, nose and body show their relationship, while colour, markings and modest build differences distinguish the variants. The creator finalized PL8B unchanged with “all final” on 2026-11-13.

- **Name:** Banded folk, because both variants carry bands. The name also avoids confusion with the Saltreach, a human coastal culture.
- **Variants:** the salt variant lives on salt flats and dry pans, and the silt variant on floodplains and river deltas. Children take their family's variant, and moving doesn't change it.
- **Alignments:** mostly Fervor, in free caravan bands on the salt roads, with some Genesis on the floodplains.
- **Temperament:** very independent, strong, hardy and aggressive, but never reckless. An orc or goblin might fly into a rage and wreck things for the joy of it; Banded folk don't.
- **Trade:** salt off the pans and grain off the floodplains bring the two variants together on the salt roads.
- **Caravans:** each is a family band, and they elect the head of the caravan.
- **Mixed families:** a salt–silt couple's children can be either variant, so siblings can differ.
- **Conflict:** Warhorde bands raid their caravans, and Banded folk look down on the Warhorde's rages.
- **Magic:** their casters are practical and travel with the caravans, reading and turning the weather. This is learned.
- **Individuals:** some hire out as guards in the cities, where their steadiness is prized.
- **Story fit:** the Fervor campaign's salt pan ("The salt was free last year") is their land, and the Ashen Court's new price is on their salt.

## What people believe about the gods

People disagree about why the gods went quiet:
- They are dead.
- They never existed.
- They still exist and are silent out of disappointment.
- They still exist and are silent for benevolent reasons.

## The truth about the gods

This is for the creator only, and no one in the world knows it.

The gods are alive, in another realm. The rope that pulled away at the Cataclysm is really a great distance in an interdimensional sense, and primordial chaos now fills the gap. The image is Satan crossing the void from Hell in *Paradise Lost* and seeing Heaven in the distance. The distance isn't infinite, but crossing it would take such strength that nobody knows if it can be done.

The gods are diminished only because they are so far away. Close to their worshippers, they grow strong. Crossing or closing that distance would make them less diminished, but it may come at a great cost.

Summoners draw their power from this. The thread that once bound them to the gods is somehow still connected, and it really leads into that primordial realm. A summoner might feel a connection to the swirling chaos and the entities within it, to goodness, or to evil entities directly. None of them knows this is what they are feeling.

**Canonical visual reference (2026-11-06):** [The Unmeasured Distance](art/campaign-2026-11-05/20-experiment-unmeasured-distance.png), the gold strand crossing blue and magenta space, is approved by the creator as canon. It depicts the surviving connection and immense separation described here. This approval does not settle the cause of the Cataclysm, establish a successful crossing, or give characters knowledge they do not have.

### What snapped

This isn't decided yet. The creator's ideas so far centre on the main god being nearly destroyed and heavily diminished:
- He gave himself as a sacrifice to keep the world from breaking.
- Another god betrayed him.
- The Empire developed evil technology or forbidden secrets that destroyed him.
- Cultists summoned a great primordial entity that destroyed him.

**Proposed:** a combined answer. The Empire's forbidden research let cultists reach a primordial entity, another god helped them, and the main god gave himself up to keep the world from breaking. Almost every side would share some blame, and Vigil's suspicion of Gnosis would be partly justified.

## The alignments

Alignments are outlooks and don't map neatly onto states or factions. Hybrid cards can come later. Humans appear in all of them.

**Proposed:** each alignment is partly right and doesn't know it. Vigil is right that the gods are out there but may never reach them. Gnosis is right that magic follows rules but doesn't know what it's studying. Dominion could learn the most from the evil entities in the chaos. Fervor may be closest to the chaos itself. Each campaign could reveal a different piece of the truth.

### Vigil (white)
- **Wants:** to restore the old empire and bring back the gods.
- **Believes:** the way was lost when people stopped revering the gods. It senses the gods' presence through healing magic, or at least believes it does.
- **Politics:** may favour a broad, hegemonic form of democracy, among other forms.
- **Suspects:** that Gnosis helped cause the fall by studying dark magic. This is suspicion, not outright hatred.

### Gnosis (blue)
- **Wants:** a new order. It is not opposed to organised society, but it has no interest in rebuilding the old empire.
- **Believes:** the empire stifled innovation and had stopped moving forward. Magic is a science.

### Dominion (black)
- **Life under it:** harsh and oppressive across the whole alignment. Nobody should expect a good life as a Dominion peasant. Its dark order is not a tidy civic alternative, and its places are less cosmopolitan than most.
- **Wants:** either for the degeneration to continue into utter ruin, or to build a new, dark order.
- **Politics:** the one alignment with no democratic streak.
- **Why people join (creator direction, 2026-11-13):** never for good reasons. Either they hold a religious belief that the world should be destroyed, or they have power over the weak in their society and enjoy it. Dominion is cruel, including to its own, and it is no refuge for outcasts.

### Fervor (red)
- **Wants:** to keep the freedom it has found in the chaos, and to prevent the empire's return.
- **Politics:** prefers smaller bands with democratic streaks, closer to a Cossack host than to a modern state.

### Genesis (green)
- **Society:** Genesis has societies and settlements, including villages in forests and homes in trees. Its inhabitants are not all tree people or animals; humans, households and community life belong here too. Specific village designs and customs remain open.
- **Wants:** to live in nature, in small bands, with no return to the empire.
- **Believes:** Gnosis would destroy the land for industry, Fervor is destructive, and Vigil is on a fool's errand.
- **At its extreme:** some of its people act as eco-terrorists.

## What each alignment's saved world looks like

**Proposed:** one idea for each alignment's campaign ending.

- **Vigil:** the empire is restored and the gods answer, at a cost that may be too much to bear.
- **Gnosis:** a new society that no longer needs the gods to answer.
- **Dominion:** peace under one dark order, or the end of everything that could suffer.
- **Fervor:** no throne ever again, and free bands across the land.
- **Genesis:** the ruins are overgrown, and people live within the wild.

## The game's shape

- **World:** Earth-like, with one large continent, some medium ones and several small ones.
- **Campaign:** one story for each alignment.
- **Maps:** each campaign has its own zoomed-in region map, mostly for atmosphere, which doesn't fix the wider geography. A larger world map is still possible. The painted world studies from 2026-11-05 aren't canon.

## Open questions

1. **Names:** the gods and the continents still need names. They can be longer than one word.
2. **The snap:** which of the ideas above, if any, is the true cause of the Cataclysm?
3. **Title:** Breachline is still a working title, and nothing in the setting explains it yet.
4. **Personal magic:** recovery rates, the relationship between replenishment and external channeling, and the injury thresholds of overexertion. The typical exhaustion progression, the ability of highly trained mages to maintain control, and the greater danger of uncontrolled raw power are established above. Training increases capacity and efficiency; species differences and the precise relationship between magic and alignment remain tentative.
5. **Stored energy:** the origin of magical materials, the details of their trade and Ambrel's possible partial control, and the behaviour of materials other than the Mountain's Lung's permanently spent mana-stones. Mana-stones remain accessible.
6. **Portals:** where they can connect and what they cost. Their rarity comes from unusual talent and difficult learned technique.
7. **Revival and spirits:** exact time thresholds and limits of partial soul restoration, the relationship between bodily repair and soul recovery, and the forms of lasting scarring. The alignments' approaches to diminished revival are established above, with Ambrel's sustaining experiments tentative. Vigil permits an immediate revival attempt only by a sufficiently strong healer. Costs, requirements, the extent of a spirit's agency and its relationship to soul loss also remain open.

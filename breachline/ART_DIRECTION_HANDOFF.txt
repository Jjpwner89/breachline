Accepted painting approach
Read ../.claude/skills/art-direction/SKILL.md from the game folder's parent repository, and SETTING.md before briefing. The skill contains the reusable rules; HISTORY.md contains past decisions.

Use coherent directional lighting, readable faces and figures, lively colour and deliberately varied brushwork/detail. Light and magic should support the painting without taking over. Do not make every alignment dark, grey or low-contrast to imitate Dominion. Keep matte skin, cloth, wood and stone; reserve stronger reflections for metal, glass and water. Use selective sharpness and broader, quieter secondary forms.

The scene should make sense without its title or rules. Gaze, gesture, object contact, equipment and location should fit together. A still compelling person needs no invented task. Work should look practical rather than heroic. Check connected hands/feet and ground support. Bows need credible scale, grip, string and nocked-arrow geometry.

Recommended brief division — assistant recommendation, discussed with creator
The creator asked whether Claude's briefs should be pared down. My recommendation is a lighter brief, not mechanics alone. Claude should supply the card's identity, lore, role in the game, one concise narrative/emotional idea, and any non-negotiable visual constraints or references. Keep mechanics separately as context, without demanding literal visualization.

The art agent should choose composition, viewpoint, lighting and paint handling using the shared art direction. Avoid duplicating style prescriptions in every card brief or letting mechanics dictate the action. Habitat, anatomy, identity and required equipment are useful constraints; a forced pose, magical effect or heroic spectacle usually is not.

Some earlier overdirection came from this art agent's own expansion—particularly “a clearly different dramatic composition” for every card—rather than necessarily from Claude. The workflow recommendation is not a unilateral change to Claude's brief-writing instructions. A future fresh-chat test should check whether the shared art rules transfer without attaching finished paintings merely as style examples.
